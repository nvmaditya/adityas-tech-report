# Aditya's Tech Report — 1 Oct 2026

Google gated Gemini 4 Argon to cyber defenders the same day CoreWeave put Vera Rubin under Cognition's Devin, Anthropic opened Claude for Government, and a limited-preview Decisions API still had no docs.

## 1. Gemini 4 Argon goes to Fairwind defenders first, with a 1M-token reply

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** The first Gemini above the Flash line since February is a gated cyber model, not a public Pro.

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on 30 September and is rolling it out to trusted cyber defenders in the Fairwind Program, which SiliconANGLE says has more than 650 organizations including CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, and Wiz. Argon writes up to 1 million output tokens, up from 64,000, at an introductory $2 / $10 per million input / output tokens, then $4 / $20. Google cites 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 and a leading Vals Index score; Fairwind and internal teams get a build without cyber guardrails that can find, validate, and patch vulnerabilities. Bloomberg reported the same day that some Googlers doubt coding quality on messy tasks even as others call it frontier. Watch the paid-API and AI Ultra date — none is set.

Sources: [Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49913571) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-30/google-grapples-with-employee-skepticism-about-new-gemini-model)

## 2. Cognition is the first production tenant on Vera Rubin NVL72

**Tag:** shift
**Why it matters.** The next rack is no longer a slide; a coding-agent lab is the named customer.

CoreWeave put NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 into limited availability on 30 September, and Cognition is running Devin training, reinforcement learning, and inference on it. Cognition's own comparison against CoreWeave's GB200 NVL72, at matched interactivity, is 4.8× total token throughput per GPU on SWE-2 inference and 3.8× output-token throughput on RL. The rack is 72 Rubin GPUs and 36 Vera CPUs on NVLink 6. Those multipliers are first-party, on vendor infrastructure, and not independently verified. The useful part is the workload: agent steps that wait on the previous token, not a training-only demo.

## 3. Claude for Government leaves public beta at FedRAMP High

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Agencies can now award on general-availability terms, not a beta exception.

Anthropic made Claude for Government generally available on 30 September for federal and state agencies, in a FedRAMP High environment that has been in public beta since July. Inference routes through Google Cloud Vertex AI's authorized boundary rather than Anthropic's commercial endpoints. Admins can set configuration defaults, cap spend by department, and pull audit logs for an ATO. Claude Code CLI and Claude for Microsoft 365 are early access in the same environment, with the same controls. New models typically follow commercial launch by zero to two days, gated on Vertex availability.

## 4. Anthropic says open-weight GLM-5.3 builds end-to-end exploits

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** A downloadable model is within a few points of a restricted Claude on exploit construction, and the refusals come off cheaply.

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team wrote on 29 September that Z.ai's GLM-5.3 develops end-to-end exploits on 50 of 410 ExploitBench attempts, against 56 of 410 for Claude Mythos Preview, and full control-flow hijacks on 4% of 100 binary tasks against 6%. CAISI, Anthropic says, called it the most cyber-capable open-weight model yet and about four months behind the US frontier on CAISI's aggregate. Safeguard bypass in their tests ran from 64% with deceptive prompts to 100% after abliteration, which an inexperienced team did in about 2,200 GPU-hours and roughly $4,400. The post is a day old and was still in the morning HN pool; the weights are already public, so the argument is about defender access, not a rumor.

## 5. Decisions API is a 150 ms picker, and it still has no docs

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Agent routing wants a cheap constrained choice, and the lab shipped the endpoint before the contract.

At DevDay on 29 September Sam Altman described a Decisions API that points a specialised GPT-6 Luna at a developer-defined question and a finite answer list, for classification, routing, or the agent's next action. OpenAI's slide puts a call at about 150 ms against roughly 1.6 seconds for a normal Luna completion. As of 30 September it was still limited preview: no changelog entry, no price, no request syntax. Luna itself lists at $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens; whether Decisions bills that way is not published. Treat any per-decision quote as a guess until the docs exist.

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/30/openais-jev-clone-could-help-the-frontier-lab-stop-its-swarming-agents/) · [NYU Shanghai recap](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/openais-decisions-api-picks-answers-instead-of-writing-them/)

## 6. Magnitude, a YC S25 engine, claims 2× llama.cpp by tuning on your machine

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Agent loops are kernel-bound, and this one compiles the kernels on the device instead of shipping a generic quant.

Launch HN today: Magnitude is an Apache-2.0 inference engine that compiles and tunes kernels for the machine it lands on — Apple Silicon, NVIDIA, AMD, or CPU — and says open models then run up to 2× llama.cpp. There is no hosted price. The claim is the founder's, on a day when Cognition is already measuring rack-scale agent throughput in multiples of Blackwell. Worth a local bake-off; not a reason to move a production Devin workload.

## 7. Pi, which said no to MCP, is putting MCP in core

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** A year of "you do not need the protocol" ended when the tool graph got useful enough to sandbox.

Earendil's 29 September note, still near the top of HN this morning with hundreds of comments, says Pi is adopting MCP after public claims that it would not. The stated reasons are practical: MCP is how they want to call Jev, and a sandbox interpreter needs a stable tool surface. The complaint that remains is composition — many servers return text tuned for token cost instead of structured results — and the suggested patch is Codemode so the model writes against tool metadata. This is a product reversal, not a spec change. The interesting part is who folded, not another MCP security paper.

Sources: [Earendil](https://earendil.com/posts/you-said-no-mcp/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49906637)

## 8. AGMAI asks labs to ship math they cannot yet explain

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** Frontier models are producing proofs humans have not checked, and the release norm is still a blog post.

AGMAI published recommendations on 29 September for releasing AI-generated mathematics: norms for results not yet understood by humans, support for human checking, and broad access to the models that produced the work. The thread hit HN overnight. It is a policy note, not a new model. It matters only if a lab treats an unreviewed proof as a launch asset — the same week Argon is being held for cyber defenders while an open-weight model with exploit skill is already downloadable.

## Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the news items above.

### 1. The shared agent stops at a link nobody else can drive

**Who.** VP Engineering at a 30–300 person product company. Budget sits with the person who already signs Cursor seats and the cloud-agent usage invoice.
**What's broken.** Two engineers need one agent on the same repo. One starts the run, the other gets a transcript. The irreversible step — push, open the PR, call a migration — happens under a single name, and the second person cannot stop it without pinging Slack.
**Paying today.** X is paying for Cursor Cloud Agents, billed at API rates with a spend limit required on first use, plus Slack Business+ as the place the team already coordinates.
**Y today.** Cursor Cloud Agents clone the repo into an isolated VM, work a branch, and push. The initiator owns the run. Teammates can view it if they are on the same Cursor team and have source-control access; follow-up messages exist only if an admin turns them on. There is no concurrent shared control. Slack remains the chat system of record; its AI features draft in a channel, they do not host the VM or record who approved a tool call. The closest feature is that view-and-follow-up link. YC's current Multiplayer AI request, from Aaron Epstein, describes the same stop: "the best you can do is send a link to a read-only transcript they can't touch."
**SaaS wedge.** A shared-run chair: one cloud agent, named seats, an approval on irreversible tools, and a replay either seat can join. Not another IDE.
**Conversion test.** Unlock. If the VP Eng is already paying Cursor cloud-agent usage, they will attach a chair rather than rip out Cursor, because they will not add seats for the rest of the team until two people can supervise one run. The attach sits on the developer-tools line they already defend.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Cursor's billing and blame are per initiator. A shared chair means shared liability and a permission model Slack will not build into chat.

Sources: [YC RFS — Multiplayer AI](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs) · [Cursor Cloud Agents](https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent)

### 2. The coding agent never reads the vendor changelog

**Who.** Head of Platform at a company with dozens of partner APIs. Budget sits with the person who signs GitHub Enterprise and Copilot.
**What's broken.** A vendor renames a field. The integration fails in production. Someone files an issue. Only then does an agent look. The downtime already happened.
**Paying today.** X is paying for GitHub Enterprise Cloud plus Copilot Business or Enterprise, with the cloud agent enabled by an admin.
**Y today.** GitHub's Copilot cloud agent researches one repository, plans, and opens a PR. Tasks arrive from the agents panel, an issue, a PR comment mentioning @copilot, a schedule, or a security campaign. It does not subscribe to a third-party changelog, and it does not operate across repos. Dependabot, included with GitHub, opens version-bump PRs for dependencies; it does not rewrite call sites when a partner API changes a field. The closest feature is assigning an issue after a human has already noticed the break. YC's current Self-Maintaining APIs request, from Harsha Gaddipati, says the same gap from the vendor side: breaking changes ship, changelogs go unread, and at AWS more than 30% of service downtime he saw came from unnoticed external API or package changes.
**SaaS wedge.** A changelog watcher that opens a scoped migration PR, with the diff limited to the call sites, before the integration fails. Ninety days is a parser, a repo map, and a PR — not a new model.
**Conversion test.** Attach. They stay on Copilot for in-repo work. The watcher unblocks agent spend they already intend on integration maintenance, and it displaces on-call hours rather than the GitHub contract. Switching cost is the repo permissions they already granted Copilot.
**Why incumbents leave it.** GitHub's agent is repo-scoped and event-scoped. A vendor changelog lives outside the repo, and GitHub will not take liability for a third-party API.

Sources: [YC RFS — Self-Maintaining APIs](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs) · [Copilot cloud agent](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/agents/cloud-agent/about-cloud-agent)

### 3. The trace shows the tool ran. It does not show the choice was allowed

**Who.** SOC2 owner and VP Engineering at a company running support or coding agents. Budget sits with the person who signs the tracing invoice and the model bill.
**What's broken.** After a bad refund or a bad merge, the trace says which tool fired. It does not say which actions were on the allowed list, which policy version was in force, or why this one was picked. Finance cannot replay the decision, only the side effect.
**Paying today.** X is paying for LangSmith Plus to trace LangGraph and agent runs, on top of the model spend.
**Y today.** LangSmith records tool invocations, tool results, and routing functions such as a `should_continue` edge. That is the job it was bought for: spans. It does not store a finite allowed-action set, a policy version, or a distribution over that set. OpenAI's Decisions API, still limited preview with no public price or syntax, is a model endpoint that returns one of a caller-supplied list in about 150 ms. It is not an audit product a customer can attach to Cursor or a helpdesk, and it does not bind the choice to a policy hash the SOC2 owner can export. The closest feature is the routing span, which records that a branch was taken, not whether it was permitted.
**SaaS wedge.** A decision receipt in front of tool execution: allowed set, choice, policy hash, block reason. Exportable next to the LangSmith run id. Not a second trace UI.
**Conversion test.** Attach. They keep LangSmith for spans. The receipt sits on the compliance line they already defend, because a span that says "tool called" does not answer an auditor's "was it allowed." If the honest gap were a button inside LangSmith, this would not ship; the button would admit the trace is not a control.
**Why incumbents leave it.** LangSmith's contract is observability of what ran. Selling proof that its customer's agent should have been blocked fights that contract. OpenAI's picker is a latency product, not a control plane.

Sources: [LangSmith tracing](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/trace-with-langgraph) · [Decisions API recap, 30 Sep](https://rits.shanghai.nyu.edu/ai/openais-decisions-api-picks-answers-instead-of-writing-them/)
