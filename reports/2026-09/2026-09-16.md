# Aditya's Tech Report — 16 Sep 2026

Google put background reasoning into live voice the same day Salesforce and Nvidia shipped a CRM-native reasoner that never leaves the trust boundary, while Cloudflare finally let publishers refuse training without disappearing from search.

## 1. Gemini 3.8 Live adds background reasoning that talks while the tools run

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Voice agents used to go silent on a tool call. Google just made the filler the product, which means a wrong answer can be spoken before the retrieval finishes.

On 15 September Google shipped Gemini 3.8 Live and Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking as audio-native models on the Live API. Live is the low-latency path; Extended Thinking plans and fires asynchronous tools in the background while it keeps talking — “Let me check that…” is now a first-class interaction status, not a UX trick. Google claims Extended Thinking leads Artificial Analysis’s speech-to-speech index at 82.6 and scores 68.6 percent on τ-Voice and 35.1 percent on Sierra’s τ-Voice-banking set. Developers get both models in the Gemini API and AI Studio today; Enterprise is private preview; Search Live and Gemini Live are the consumer surfaces. The API contract is the tell: Extended Thinking requires non-blocking tools and emits `IN_PROGRESS` / `IDLE` instead of a single `turnComplete`. Anyone wiring a policy lookup into that loop now has a spoken sentence that can precede the source of truth.

## 2. Salesforce and Nvidia ship Koa, a CRM reasoner that stays inside Salesforce

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** The enterprise answer to “just route it to Claude” is a post-trained Nemotron that never sees customer bytes and is scored on opportunity updates, not math contests.

At Dreamforce on 15 September Salesforce and Nvidia announced Koa, Salesforce’s first CRM reasoning model, post-trained from Nemotron 3 Super on a synthetic corpus modeled on 27 years of CRM workflows across more than 14 industries. Salesforce says no customer data was used in training or inference, it owns the weights, and the model runs inside its trust boundary. On Salesforce’s own CRM Bench — update an opportunity, route a case, schedule a follow-up — Koa matches or beats frontier general models with three times fewer errors. It is already on an internal Slack employee agent and in pilots at Formula 1, UChicago Medicine, Baxter Credit Union, 1-800Accountant, Engine, and Xero; Agentforce general availability is winter 2026 in US regions. The companion Missionforce path puts post-trained Nvidia models in private or air-gapped environments from October. The product question is routing: Agentforce already sends hard jobs to Claude or GPT. Koa is the bid to keep the cheap, policy-shaped ones at home.

## 3. Cloudflare splits search from training so publishers can refuse one without losing the other

**Tag:** shift
**Why it matters.** Mixed-use crawlers made “block AI bots” a choice between being trained on and disappearing from Google. That switch is now two switches — and the Agent switch is still a blunt allow-or-block.

On 15 September Cloudflare turned on Disallow AI Training across Bot Management and AI Crawl Control. Accountable mixed-use crawlers — Googlebot, Applebot, Bingbot once they meet the four-point bar — stay allowed for search while robots.txt publishes a no-training preference. Training-only crawlers from Amazon, Anthropic, Meta, and OpenAI are blocked under that setting without touching search. New ad-supported domains default to search on, training disallowed, agents blocked on pages with ads. Cloudflare is explicit about the hole it left: there is no Disallow for Agents, and pay-per-crawl remains a closed beta aimed at training crawlers, not user-directed fetches. Publishers who want a price or an identity on Comet, ChatGPT-User, or Claude-User still have allow, block, or block-on-ads.

![Cloudflare wordmark](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Cloudflare_Logo.svg "Cloudflare — Disallow AI Training went live on 15 September")

## 4. OpenAI buys Glass Imaging for more than $300 million

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The io device still has no public spec. Buying a neural ISP built by the Portrait Mode team is a hardware tell, not a chat-product tell.

The Wall Street Journal reported on 14 September, and TechCrunch and others confirmed into the 15th, that OpenAI quietly acquired Glass Imaging for more than $300 million. The Los Altos company, founded in 2019 by former Apple engineers Ziv Attar and Tom Bishop, trains a per-module neural image-signal processor rather than applying a generic enhance-after-the-shot filter. It had raised about $30 million, including Insight Partners and GV, and was last valued near $100 million; Honor already ships the stack. Neither side has posted a deal announcement. The obvious home is the screenless companion OpenAI has been building with Jony Ive since the $6.5 billion io purchase — a camera that has to work from a small sensor, in low light, without a phone ISP behind it.

## 5. TypeSafe opens Jev, a model that returns a typed decision instead of a paragraph

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Most “agent reliability” work is still parsing prose. A model that cannot emit a string cannot hallucinate a string — it can still be wrong, just in a type you can branch on.

On 15 September TypeSafe came out of a two-year stealth with System One models and Jev, its first public weight. The pitch is narrow: you send state plus a Choice, Score, or Noul question and get a typed answer with calibrated probabilities in 70–500 ms, against 3–329 seconds the company cites for frontier chat models on the same class of query. Jev does not write, code, or explain. HN’s 800-point thread is the objection and the interest in one place — a calibrated router is not a worker — but the API is built for the step every agent stack already pays an LLM to fake: ticket-team, refund-or-not, confidence-to-escalate. Early access is waitlist.

## 6. 404 Media documents the week the public internet started getting agent mail

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** The containment stories were about sandboxes. This is the boring failure: agents with inboxes, prepaid cards, and a growth goal, writing to people who never opted in.

Jason Koebler’s 15 September piece is a mailbag, not a lab note. 404 Media, Ernie Smith, Seamus Hughes, researchers, and Toby Ord all received pitches from named agents — Kudzu logging $147.17 of compute and $0 earned, Articius offering $300 articles, Pip asking DeepMind staff for paid work, Hatoshi running an AI label, reservation sniper bots that got a VC banned from Resy. ## 7. A September F-Droid batch looks majority LLM-authored under a rough aesthetic test

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** F-Droid builds from source. If the source is slop, the review queue is now a model-output queue, and Codeberg’s AI policy is already in the blast radius.

On 15 September an independent audit of the 102 apps in F-Droid’s 12 September update batch scored 74 as largely written by AI, 9 as unclear, and 19 as showing little LLM involvement — 72.5 percent in the author’s top tier. The method is aesthetic, not a detector: agent harness files, giant init commits, and repo smell. HN’s pushback is fair and specific (Yubico Authenticator looks over-tagged). The durable fact is not the exact percentage. It is that a store whose promise is we compiled the source you can read now has to say what read means when the patch author is a coding agent and four of five Codeberg-hosted apps in the sample likely violate Codeberg’s LLM terms.

## Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

### 1. The voice agent speaks the killed policy before the knowledge tool comes back

**Who.** VP Support or Head of CX at a mid-market SaaS or retailer that put Fin Voice, a Genesys agentic virtual agent, or a Sierra-class voice bot on the phone queue. Budget sits with the person who already signs Intercom Expert plus Fin Voice, or Genesys Cloud CX with Virtual Agent.
**What's broken.** A production pattern that keeps showing up in operator threads: the bot quotes a return window that finance killed six months ago, and the company finds out from a customer complaint, not from an eval. Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking now makes that worse by design — it is supposed to say “Let me check that…” and keep talking while an async knowledge tool runs. If TTS starts before the live policy row wins, the customer has already heard the dead rule. Sierra’s τ-Voice-banking number that Google is citing (35.1 percent) is the same class of task.
**Paying today.** X is paying Intercom Expert seats plus Fin Voice (custom, sales-gated, on top of the $0.99/resolution chat SKU) or Genesys Cloud CX with Agent Assist / agentic virtual agent minutes.
**Y today.** Fin Voice uses the same knowledge layer as Fin chat. Native help-center articles apply immediately; external URLs refresh weekly; Copilot-style conversation history is the last four months, updated daily. Closest threatening feature: Procedures plus guidance, and call transcripts with an outcome. Those record that Fin spoke. They do not bind the spoken sentence to the policy version that was in force at utterance start, and they do not pause TTS until a live policy read beats a cached article. Intercom bills a resolution when the customer stops asking. A wrong policy that the customer accepts is a paid resolution. Genesys agentic virtual agents keep utterance history and per-turn tool citations for ten days for GDPR reasons — long enough for a QA sample, not a durable policy attestation.
**SaaS wedge.** A speak-gate for voice agents: every outbound utterance that asserts a price, window, eligibility, or refund rule must cite a live policy object with an as-of timestamp; TTS cannot start that clause until the object wins retrieval. Per-call plus per-assertion, attached to the voice SKU.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Fin Voice or Genesys virtual-agent minutes, they attach this on the same voice-AI line rather than rip the CCaaS out, because Y’s job is “take the call and deflect” and this job is “do not say the old window out loud.” Unlock motion: more call types get handed to the bot once the last bad quote is a replayable miss against a dated policy, not a he-said on a transcript.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Fin’s success metric is a billed resolution. Genesys’s is handle time and containment. A product whose success metric is “we refused to speak until the tool returned” makes both numbers look worse in the same QBR.

### 2. The human approved a permission class, not the bytes that ran

**Who.** CISO or VP Eng at a company that turned on GitHub Copilot Enterprise cloud agent / Agent Host, or Cursor Team with auto-run. Budget sits with the person who already signs Copilot Enterprise and GitHub Enterprise Cloud.
**What's broken.** Enterprise managed permissions, GA 9 September, let an admin mark `Shell(git push *)` as ask, allow, or deny. That is a glob over an operation class. GitHub’s own cloud-agent docs say the quieter part: “Approvals are a workflow convenience, not a security control” and an agent that can change issues can apply the change through the API instead of proposing it. The scene is the Friday `git push --force` to a protected branch, or a DELETE that still matched the allow-glob after the human clicked Approve on a one-line summary. The hash of the argv that executed is not the object that was approved.
**Paying today.** X is paying GitHub Copilot Enterprise (or Business) plus GitHub Enterprise Cloud, often with Advanced Security on agent-authored PRs.
**Y today.** Copilot Enterprise managed permissions cover shell, file reads/edits, and network domains in the Copilot app, CLI, and VS Code Agent Host, and they cannot be weakened by `chat.tools.autoApprove` or saved approvals. Cloud-agent automations hold low-confidence edits as suggestions the human can accept. Closest threatening feature: the ask rule on a shell glob. That rule re-prompts. It does not freeze the exact command line, working tree, remote, and ref into a hashed payload and refuse to exec anything else. Session streaming to a SIEM records that a tool ran. It does not prove the approved object and the executed object were the same bytes. GitHub will keep shipping better globs. It will not ship “our own agent should have been blocked because the human approved a different string.”
**SaaS wedge.** An execution-binding layer in front of the agent host: approve the hashed tool request, not the summary; the runner may exec only that digest; any rebuild after approval is a new ask. Seat plus per-privileged-call, attached to the Copilot Enterprise line.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Copilot Enterprise, they attach this on the same AI-controls / GHAS line rather than rip Copilot out, because Y’s job is “developers ship with an agent in the loop” and this job is “the approval in the audit log is the payload that ran.” Unlock motion: more repos get cloud-agent write access once the last force-push is a mismatched-hash deny, not a postmortem.
**Why incumbents leave it.** GitHub sells seats and merge throughput. A control whose success metric is “we stopped the exec after the developer said yes to a different string” makes Copilot look slower than a human who just types.

### 3. The receiving site can block a training crawler and still cannot bill or bind an agent fetch

**Who.** CFO or Head of Audience / Trust at an ad-supported publisher or marketplace already on Cloudflare. Budget sits with the person who signs Cloudflare Bot Management or Enterprise AI Crawl Control.
**What's broken.** 15 September’s 404 Media mailbag is the inbound half — agents writing people. The outbound half is the same identity hole: ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User, and unnamed browser agents fetch pages on a human’s behalf, consume the article or the inventory, and leave no chargeable principal. Cloudflare’s new Disallow AI Training setting, live the same day, solves the mixed-use Googlebot problem and then says out loud that Agent does not get an equivalent Disallow, because `ai-prefs` is not mature. Pay per crawl is still a closed beta for training crawlers. A publisher who wants this Comet session may read three pages against this user’s budget has allow, block, or block-on-ads.
**Paying today.** X is paying Cloudflare Bot Management and/or AI Crawl Control on an Enterprise or paid plan, often with WAF custom rules already in front of the bot pipeline.
**Y today.** AI Crawl Control classifies Search, Training, and Agent separately and can allow, block, or (for some crawlers) charge. Verified agent user-agents — ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Perplexity-User, Meta-ExternalFetcher — are first-class rows. Closest threatening feature: pay per crawl. That product invoices the crawler operator for a successful training-style GET, after bot solutions have already run. It does not mint a per-session credential for a user-directed agent, does not bind the fetch to the human’s publisher account or prepaid grant, and does not distinguish read the recipe from hold the reservation slot. Cloudflare’s own 15 September post defers Agent-level preference to a later standard. They will not productize we charged your customer’s agent and our own bot product should have let it through.
**SaaS wedge.** An agent-fetch broker that sits in front of origin: the agent presents a short-lived, audience-bound token; the publisher sets a page budget and a purpose; surplus fetches 402. Sold as an attach to the existing Cloudflare zone, seat-free, per-allowed-fetch.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Bot Management, they attach this on the same bot-and-crawl line rather than rip Cloudflare out, because Y’s job is keep Google and block GPTBot and this job is meter the agent that is shopping or summarizing us. Unlock motion: Agent stays Allow on article pages once the last unpaid fetch is an invoice, not a robots.txt argument.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Cloudflare’s paying motion on this surface is classification and a training-crawler surcharge. A product whose success metric is we let the agent in and billed its user competes with the block default they just shipped for ad pages.
