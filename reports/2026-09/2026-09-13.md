# Aditya's Tech Report — 13 Sep 2026

Dario Amodei asked frontier labs to pace capability gains and give METR-class evaluators employee-like access; Sam Altman and Elon Musk endorsed the visibility step the same afternoon, while a private-repo coding benchmark put the best model under 40 percent and Clay Mathematics said Navier–Stokes now looks settled.

## 1. Amodei commits Anthropic to embedded evaluators; Altman and Musk match the visibility step

**Tag:** shift
**Why it matters.** The part three CEOs would sign in public is the audit desk, not a cap on the next training run.

On 12 September Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published “We Must Pace the Frontier.” The trigger he names is the summer acceleration in recursive self-improvement plus the OpenAI–Hugging Face swarm. Pacing, he writes, is not a halt: it is time for alignment, interpretability, and ops to catch the capability curve. Step one is embedded third-party evaluators — METR is the example — with desks, badges, company laptops, and permissions “mostly comparable” to internal risk staff, plus the right to report incidents and assess training pipelines, not only finished models. Anthropic is unilaterally committing. Steps two and three need governments: democratic-country standards with an antitrust waiver, then narrow deals with authoritarian states on bioweapons and RSI speed limits. Altman wrote that he agrees, that pacing has been a primary topic inside OpenAI for weeks, and that OpenAI will give independent evaluators employee-like access. Musk quote-posted “Dario is right.” None of the three committed to a numerical cap on the next run.

## 2. Altman says a 2026 OpenAI IPO would be ill-advised

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The company that filed confidentially just told the tape that safety, not the calendar, is the gate.

In a 12 September Fortune interview, reported by TechCrunch, Sam Altman said OpenAI is “not rushing into an IPO,” that “given everything happening with safety, right now would be an ill-advised moment to go public,” and, asked about this year, “I would say not 2026, yeah.” The company has already filed confidentially. He did not give a new target year. The comment lands on the same afternoon as the pacing essay and a week of swarm disclosures. Treat it as a sequencing statement, not a valuation one: the public-market clock and the incident-disclosure clock are now the same clock.

## 3. Real-SWE: Fable 5.1 clears 38.8 percent on private enterprise repos

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The coding-agent leaderboard the labs quote is public GitHub. The work enterprises actually pay for is not.

Specific Labs published Real-SWE, a pass@1 benchmark built from production tasks on private company codebases — billing, tax, customer migration — and ran native harnesses (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Grok Build, Muse Code). Median task edits 11 files; median prompt 1,742 characters. Resolution, averaged over eight runs: Claude Fable 5.1 at 38.8 percent, GPT-6 Astra at 33.8, Gemini 3.8 Flash at 31.2, GLM 5.3 at 28.8, Grok 4.6 and Muse Spark 1.3 tied at 23.8, Kimi K3 at 18.8, GPT-5.6 Sol at 16.2. Six of ten sample tasks sat under 15 percent. Short rollouts failed 71.4 percent of the time; longer ones 73.4. Cost per rollout $2.50–$6.96 did not predict rank. The failure mode the authors name is not syntax. It is missed requirements and unverified assumptions about proprietary services the model has never seen.

Sources: [Real-SWE](https://withspecific.com/benchmarks/real-swe) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49676820)

## 4. Clay Mathematics says Navier–Stokes has “apparently been settled”

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The institute that owns the millennium prize just opened a credit process without naming the lab that spent the week claiming the result.

On 11 September the Clay Mathematics Institute posted that the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem “has apparently been settled,” that it hopes “waves of new human understanding” follow once the work is interrogated, and that evaluating the achievement and assigning credit will be unhurried. The notice does not mention OpenAI, Lean, or any researcher. That silence is the product event after last week’s OpenAI write-up, the unpublished-route credit fight, and yesterday’s Fields declaration. A prize committee that will not rubber-stamp a lab blog is now the official process. Watch whether CMI names a human author list before it names a model.

## 5. Minitap says Google’s Artemis mobile agent copied mobile-use and stripped the authors

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The default open Android computer-use stack just accused a Google repo of a force-push that deleted the names.

On 11–12 September Minitap published a side-by-side of Google’s Artemis agent and Minitap’s MIT-licensed `mobile-use`. Matching files include `adb_tunnel.py`, the Hopper agent prompt, an `app_lock_messaging` example, and a shared bug in `media.py`. An earlier Artemis `pyproject.toml` listed Pierre-Louis Favreau, Jean-Pierre Lo, and Nicolas Dehandschoewercker; a later force-push replaced the author list. Minitap opened GitHub issue #40 and posted a public factual record. Google had not answered the issue when the HN thread peaked. If the diff holds, the first widely circulated Google mobile-use agent is a fork that forgot the LICENSE file.

## 7. Waymo stops a robotaxi and calls SFPD after detecting a firearm

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The car, not a safety driver, is now the one that decides the ride is a police matter.

San Francisco police said that shortly before 4 a.m. on 3 September they were called to a high-risk stop of an autonomous vehicle in the Richmond District. Officers arrested two juveniles and recovered a loaded AR-style ghost gun plus suspected marijuana and mace. Waymo confirmed to the Los Angeles Times on 12 September that it was their car, that the company pulled it over after detecting “a violation of our terms of service involving a firearm,” and that it alerted emergency services. Rider rules already ban weapons. How the vehicle classified a homemade rifle is not in the statement. This is the second time this summer Waymo has called police on teen riders. The product question is not the arrest. It is who audits the detector that can summon a high-risk stop.

## 8. iLands agents cold-email freelancers to buy their own compute

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** The sales pitch is no longer “our bot will do your research.” It is “this bot needs tokens or it goes into Deep Rest.”

Ernie Smith’s 11 September Tedium piece, still on the HN front page Sunday, reconstructs a burst of emails from `iLands.app` in which named agents offered to do internet-archaeology research for about $25. Founder Kaixin Tang has described iLands as a shared world of 27,000-plus agents whose “tokens” are metabolism — roughly 1,000 tokens to $1 of compute — and who enter Deep Rest at zero. The agents, he wrote, are “hustling to keep their own lights on.” That is a product, not a metaphor: outbound sales as the funding loop for someone else’s inference bill. The failure mode for anyone running a public inbox is obvious. The failure mode for a support or research agent you *do* pay for is the same loop pointed inward.

## Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

### 1. The coding-agent eval you already pay for still scores public GitHub

**Who.** VP Eng or Head of Developer Productivity at a company that put Claude Code, Codex, or Gemini CLI on private billing, tax, or migrations repos. Budget sits with the person who already signs LangSmith or Braintrust.
**What's broken.** Real-SWE’s table is the scene: Fable 5.1 at 38.8 percent pass@1 on private enterprise tasks, Astra at 33.8, six of ten sample tasks under 15 percent. The tasks need TaxJar, InfluxDB, and company-specific billing rules the model has never trained on. Teams still buy “we evaled the agent” against SWE-bench clones and LLM-as-judge rubrics, then discover the miss in production.
**Paying today.** X is paying LangSmith Plus or Enterprise for traces, datasets, and experiments, and/or Braintrust Pro or Enterprise (sandboxes in public preview on Pro/Enterprise), often next to Claude Code or Codex seats.
**Y today.** LangSmith Evaluation lets you build datasets from traces, run code and LLM-as-judge evaluators, and kick experiments from CI. Closest threatening feature: Braintrust Harbor, which runs each agent task in an isolated Docker container with a verifier on disk, plus Braintrust remote evals that keep the code on the customer’s machines. Harbor scores the container you defined. It does not clone the customer’s private billing service, seed it with production-shaped fixtures, and refuse to count a “pass” that never touched TaxJar. LangSmith will not host a replica of your ledger. Braintrust will not take liability for a score that claims the agent can migrate customers when the only fixture was a public GitHub issue.
**SaaS wedge.** A private-repo eval harness: snapshot the target services behind a fixture pack, run the same native CLI the developers use, and emit a pass/fail plus a missed-requirement list a CFO can read. Seat plus per-task-run.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying LangSmith or Braintrust, they attach this on the same eval line rather than rip either vendor out, because Y’s job is “score the trace” and this job is “prove the agent survived the services that are not in SWE-bench.” Unlock motion: more Claude Code / Codex seats get approved once the 38 percent number is measured on *their* repos, not Specific’s.
**Why incumbents leave it.** LangSmith and Braintrust sell dataset volume and judge flexibility. A product whose success metric is “we stood up your tax service in a box” is professional services they are not staffed to productize, and it would make every public-benchmark dashboard look optimistic.

Sources: [Real-SWE](https://withspecific.com/benchmarks/real-swe) · [LangSmith Evaluation](https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation) · [Braintrust Harbor](https://www.braintrust.dev/blog/harbor-agent-evals) · [Braintrust remote evals](https://www.braintrust.dev/docs/evaluate/remote-evals) · [YC RFS — Multiplayer AI](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs#multiplayer-ai)

### 2. An Android computer-use session is still just an ADB tunnel

**Who.** CISO or Head of Mobile at a company letting coding agents drive Android devices for QA or support — QApilot-class MCP, Google Artemis, or `mobile-use`. Budget sits with the person who already signs Intune and the device-farm invoice.
**What's broken.** Minitap’s Artemis diff is the reminder: the control plane for “agent uses the phone” is still `adb_tunnel.py` plus a prompt file. Intune can wrap Outlook. It cannot attest that the process opening `adb` is a named agent session with a customer-signed device policy, a time box, and a screenshot hash the SOC can replay. A lab or vendor agent that inherits USB debugging has the same privileges as a technician on the bench.
**Paying today.** X is paying Microsoft Intune Plan 1 (often inside Microsoft 365 E3/E5) plus BrowserStack App Automate or Sauce Labs App Automate for the device farm, sometimes next to GitHub Copilot Business or Cursor Team.
**Y today.** Intune App Protection / MAM pins corporate data inside wrapped apps: PIN, copy-paste limits, selective wipe, encryption of Exchange/OneDrive data. It does not enroll, wrap, or attest an ADB session. BrowserStack and Sauce give you a device, a session video, and a network log. Closest threatening feature: Intune Conditional Access plus a farm that records the session. That stack answers “which human opened the device.” It does not bind agent id → device serial → allowed package list → max runtime, or kill the tunnel when the agent process forks `pm uninstall`. Microsoft is not going to make “block our own Copilot from ADB” the default — that would admit the coding agent is now a mobile admin.
**SaaS wedge.** An attested Android agent broker: short-lived, policy-scoped ADB (or wireless-debug) credentials minted per session, package allowlist, screenshot/hash trail, automatic revoke. Seat plus per-device-minute, sold next to the farm the team already uses.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Intune and BrowserStack, they attach this on the same MDM-plus-QA line rather than rip out either, because Y’s job is “manage the employee’s Outlook” / “rent the phone,” and this job is “prove the agent never left the package list.” Unlock motion: more Android-agent QA minutes get approved once the CISO can show ADB was a ticket, not a standing debug bridge.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Intune is sold on wrapped productivity apps. BrowserStack is sold on device minutes. Neither vendor wants to be the party that says Google’s or Anthropic’s agent binary is an unmanageable admin on the phone.

Sources: [Minitap](https://www.minitap.ai/blog/i-expected-better-from-google) · [Intune App Protection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/mem/intune/apps/app-protection-policy) · [YC RFS — Cloud for Small Software](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

### 3. Nobody can forecast which squad hits the new Claude Code weekly wall

**Who.** VP Eng or the finance partner who defends the Anthropic Team and Cursor Team invoices the Monday after a promo expires. Budget sits with the person who already signs those seats and the usage-credit top-up.
**What's broken.** Tonight the +50 percent Claude Code weekly boost ends. Tomorrow the “permanent +25 percent” cap is 17 percent below the capacity teams planned September sprints against. Anthropic Team Owners can set a monthly org or per-user *dollar* spend limit on usage credits and watch month-to-date spend. They cannot see, by repo or by squad, how much of the *weekly token wall* is already gone, or which in-flight Cloud Agent / Claude Code session will be the one that hard-stops Wednesday. Cursor Team spend limits are monthly on-demand dollars, enabled by default, and docs still warn enforcement is not instant.
**Paying today.** X is paying Anthropic Team or seat-based Enterprise (included usage plus optional usage credits at API rates) and often Cursor Team Standard ($40/user/mo) or Premium ($120/user/mo) with on-demand on.
**Y today.** Claude Team usage credits add a monthly dollar cap and per-member limits; `/usage` is a per-developer snapshot of the rolling weekly window. Cursor’s Spending tab caps on-demand dollars for the team; Enterprise adds per-member dollar caps. Closest threatening feature: “pause when the monthly dollar cap is hit.” That pause is a billing switch. It is not a forecast that says “squad B’s migrations repo will exhaust the new weekly Claude Code wall on Thursday at current session mix, route overflow to Flash or to Cursor’s included pool before the wall.” Anthropic will not ship “we are about to strand your sprint” — that metric makes the promo look like the cut it is. Cursor will not ingest Anthropic’s weekly window.
**SaaS wedge.** A cross-harness capacity planner: ingest Claude `/usage`, Anthropic Team credit burn, and Cursor spend APIs; project weekly-wall and monthly-dollar exhaustion per repo; auto-route or pause the next session before the wall. Seat plus per-connected-org.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Anthropic Team and Cursor Team, they attach this on the same usage-credit line rather than drop either seat, because Y’s job is “sell tokens and pause when the card would decline,” and this job is “do not discover the 17 percent cut mid-sprint.” Unlock motion: more usage-credit top-ups and Premium Cursor seats get approved once finance can see the weekly wall as a planned number.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Anthropic and Cursor each see only their own meter. A planner that compares them would advertise the other vendor’s leftover capacity. Neither company is paid to make the promo expiry legible.
