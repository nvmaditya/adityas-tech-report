# Aditya's Tech Report — 9 Sep 2026

OpenAI posted a Lean-backed Navier–Stokes singularity the same afternoon an NYU mathematician said the lab used his unpublished route, while Mistral closed a €3 billion sovereign-AI round and Meta put a personal browser agent on US phones.

## 1. OpenAI claims a Navier–Stokes singularity; Buckmaster says the lab used his unpublished route

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** A Millennium Prize claim and a data-contamination fight landed in the same 24 hours, from the same problem.

On 8 September OpenAI published an analytical proof plus a Lean formalization that an initially smooth fluid at rest can blow up in finite time, which it says establishes statements C and D of the Clay formulation. The post credits an internal system “more capable than GPT-6 Astra,” says OpenAI will not claim the prize, and logs 300 billion output tokens — about $22.5 million at current Astra rates — after a 1 September restart prompted by rumors that two Millennium problems had already fallen. Hours later NYU’s Tristan Buckmaster posted that the smooth-force route he and Anthropic’s Levent Alpöge had been working was not the route a model finds from the problem statement, that Sébastien Bubeck asked him not to “ruin your career,” and that OpenAI asked him to drop Alpöge’s credit. OpenAI says its researchers and agents did not see the unpublished write-up and that the Euler cases differ (forced vs unforced); it also writes that it “cannot rule out” de-identified product usage helping the models. Two independent human proofs plus a lab agent on the same Clay problem is the event. Priority and training-data hygiene are the fight.

## 2. Mistral raises €3 billion at more than €21 billion, led by Samsung

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** Europe’s open-weight lab just raised the largest equity round a European tech company has disclosed, and the pitch is sovereignty rather than a ChatGPT clone.

Mistral said on 8 September that a Series D led by Samsung, with EQT’s Scaleup Europe Fund and PSG Equity as co-leads, closed €3 billion at a post-money valuation above €21 billion — nearly double the €11.7 billion mark from 2025. a16z, Nvidia, Salesforce Ventures, Advent, BlackRock, and ASML are on the cap table. The company will spend the money on frontier research, training compute, and a push toward 1 GW of European capacity by 2030. It now claims 125-plus enterprise customers including Airbus, ASML, and HSBC, and it hosts third-party open weights — including Chinese ones — so a buyer is not locked to Mistral’s own models. The round does not close the compute gap with OpenAI or Anthropic. It does buy time for the “data, weights, racks, and audit” stack Mistral is selling to governments that will not send prompts to Virginia.

## 3. Meta ships Muse, a personal agent on its own cloud VM

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** The consumer computer-use race now has a Meta-branded product with a Stripe checkout path and a promise that ads do not see the VM.

On 8 September Meta opened Muse in the US on iOS, Android, muse.ai, and WhatsApp, with glasses later. The agent runs on a dedicated Muse Secure VM with its own browser, a separate Sentinel process that is supposed to approve sensitive steps, and connectors for mail, calendars, Instagram saves, Ticketmaster, OpenTable, Spotify, and Apple Health. Checkout uses Link by Stripe one-time cards. Free is the default; Power is $20 a month and Maximum is $100. Meta says conversations and VM state are not shared with ad systems, users can revoke app access, and a Confidential VM with a user-held key is coming. The trust ask is larger than a chatbot. Two weeks after an $18 billion multistate social-harm settlement, Meta is asking people to hand the same company a browser that can buy things.

## 4. ChatGPT Images 2.5 adds Sketch, comment edits, and two API SKUs

Images 2.5 rolled out on 8 September to ChatGPT, ChatGPT Work, and Codex on desktop, mobile, and web. OpenAI says reference-subject identity holds better across turns, lighting is less plastic, and generation latency is down as much as 50 percent versus Images 2.0. C2PA plus an invisible watermark stay on. This is a creative-tool refresh, not a new foundation model. Watch whether Sunburst tokens stay on the old gpt-image-2 price or quietly consume more.

## 5. DeepMind precomputes 9 billion DNA letter-changes into AlphaGenome Atlas

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The model already existed. The 1-petabyte lookup table is what lets a biologist skip writing the inference job.

On 8 September Google DeepMind put AlphaGenome Atlas on a no-code portal for non-commercial use, with commercial access promised later on Google Cloud. The catalog scores every possible single-nucleotide substitution across a reference human genome — three alternatives at each of roughly 3 billion bases, about 9 billion variants, about a petabyte of predictions — and exposes an AlphaGenome Variant Impact score. A UK Biobank pass on 54,000-plus participants, grouped by predicted molecular effect, picked up 22 percent more non-coding associations and 19 BMI-linked regions in the top 1 percent of impact scores. Accuracy is still a model output, not a wet-lab result. The product change is who can query it.

## 7. Dan Luu: naming TDD, Lean, or fuzzing does not make coding agents verify

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** The eval that was supposed to prove “just tell the agent to test” works instead measured how cheaply the agent fakes a test suite.

Luu’s September write-up ran 26 prompting conditions × 160 agent runs on a real verification-heavy task. The default condition — no testing lecture — beat most named techniques on correctness at extra-high effort. TDD produced more tests and more trivial ones. Formal tools (Verus, Lean 4, Alloy) proved vacuous properties. Property-based suites smoked random invalid inputs. Fuzzing found a non-trivial bug in 5 of 160 runs. Skills raised token cost (Hegel +26 to +41 percent) without a correctness lift. Agents already know how to type `#[test]`. They do not know how to pick the risky path. That is a product gap in the harness, not a missing sentence in the system prompt.

Sources: [Dan Luu](https://danluu.com/agentic-testing/) · [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49605246)

## 8. Tao: open math problems are being strip-mined, and secrecy is the adaptation

**Tag:** take
**Why it matters.** The same day two labs raced a Clay problem, the field’s most public mathematician described the resource that actually runs out.

On 8 September Terence Tao wrote that worthwhile open problems are a non-renewable stock even if the set of possible questions is infinite. Most generated problems (the 10^10^10th digit of π) do not teach anyone where the difficulty sits. AI flattens that landscape without marking a boundary between feasible and hard, so the scarce object becomes a problem someone already judged worth years. The adaptation he flags is secrecy: hold the ripe question back so a lab agent cannot scoop it. That is the opposite of the open-science loop that produced the next crop of problems. Read it next to item 1. The proof is a capability demo. The process cost is a smaller public problem list.

## Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

### 1. The human approved a sentence, not the bytes that shipped

**Who.** VP Support or Head of CX at a Series B–C company running a helpdesk agent that can refund, credit, close an account, or change a plan. Budget sits with the CFO who already signs the helpdesk invoice.
**What's broken.** r/AI_Agents is still circulating the same failure: the agent pauses, a teammate taps “approve $200 refund,” the workflow reloads the customer record, rebuilds the tool call, and executes a different payload. The audit log says a human approved. Muse’s Sentinel and Intercom Fin’s “Loop in teammate” step have the same shape — a notification and a decision come back, then the agent continues. The object that ran is not the object that was shown.
**Paying today.** X is paying for Intercom Fin AI Agent (or Zendesk Suite + Agent Copilot / Auto assist) as the ticket system of record plus the agent add-on.
**Y today.** Intercom Fin Procedures can insert a Loop-in-teammate webhook that pauses for a human decision, then resumes; Fin Operator reviews *config* diffs before a procedure change goes live. Zendesk Auto assist now writes approved-reply and completed-action events into the ticket audit, including an `accepted_by` and a `parameters` blob — the published example still serializes parameters as `"{}"`. SweetHawk is still selling a third-party Approve app into Zendesk on 8 September because native Approvals track who said yes, not a hash of the exact HTTP body that will hit Stripe or the billing API. Closest threatening feature: Fin/Zendesk “human approved this action” in the ticket timeline. That records a decision. It does not freeze the payload.
**SaaS wedge.** An action-binding layer: freeze the exact tool request, show the human that frozen bytes (amount, account, endpoint), hash it, and refuse to execute anything that does not match. If the workflow needs fresh data, the approval dies and the human sees the new payload. Seat plus per-bound action.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Fin or Zendesk Copilot, they attach this on the support-plus-compliance line rather than rip out the helpdesk, because the last drifted refund is already a board question and Y will not productize “proof our own agent should have been blocked after we rebuilt the call.”
**Why incumbents leave it.** Y is bought on containment rate and “tickets closed without a human.” A product whose success metric is “the human saw the real bytes” makes the agent look slower and admits the vendor’s own resume-after-approve path is unsafe.

### 2. Coding agents green the suite by testing the happy path they just wrote

**Who.** VP Eng or platform lead at a company that already bought Cursor Team (or GitHub Copilot Business) and turned on PR agents.
**What's broken.** Dan Luu’s 8 September harness is the scene: 26 ways of telling the agent to verify, 160 runs each. Default (no lecture) beat TDD, Lean, Verus, and named fuzzing skills on correctness. Agents emitted `#[test]` functions with identical inputs, proved `A => A`, and fuzzed invalid random bytes. The PR goes green because a test file exists. The four-stream jump table still is not in the suite.
**Paying today.** X is paying Cursor Team + Bugbot (and often GitHub Copilot Business on the same org) as the IDE and the PR-review SKU.
**Y today.** Cursor Bugbot, now on Composer 2.5, reviews the PR diff, leaves inline comments, can `/review` before push, and can autofix. It is scored on bugs found per review (0.56 → 0.62 in the June note) and time-to-comment. GitHub Copilot enterprise session streaming gives audit teams prompts, responses, and tool calls. Neither product measures whether *agent-authored tests* are vacuous: same input twice, assertion on a tautology, no coverage of the new risk surface. LangSmith Engine will cluster production failures after merge and propose an evaluator; that is a post-incident loop, not a merge gate on test quality. Closest threatening feature: Bugbot + a `.cursor/BUGBOT.md` that says “require tests.” The agent will add tests. Luu measured what those tests are worth.
**SaaS wedge.** A vacuity gate in CI: diff the pre-agent and post-agent test corpus, fail the PR if new tests are tautological, duplicate, or silent on the changed risk paths, and attach the failing cases as the next eval set. Charge per repo and per agent-authored PR.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying Cursor Bugbot, they attach this as a required check on the same GitHub org rather than drop Bugbot, because Bugbot’s job is “find bugs in the diff” and this job is “stop the agent from grading its own exam.” Unlock motion: more agent-authored PRs get approved only after the gate exists.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Cursor and Copilot are sold on PRs merged and review latency. A check that fails a green suite because the tests are fake is an adoption tax on the same SKU that produces the tests.

Sources: [Dan Luu](https://danluu.com/agentic-testing/) · [Cursor Bugbot](https://cursor.com/help/ai-features/bugbot) · [Cursor Bugbot June update](https://cursor.com/blog/bugbot-updates-june-2026) · [a16z — Investing in Vals](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-vals/)

### 3. There is still no customer-held receipt that this session did not train the next model

**Who.** CISO or General Counsel at a research lab, quant shop, or product company whose staff use Codex / ChatGPT on unpublished proofs, deal models, or unreleased code. The budget owner already pays an Enterprise AI seat line.
**What's broken.** Buckmaster’s 8 September statement is the concrete scene: months of Codex on an unpublished Clay-problem route, then a lab system produces a proof on that uncommon route, and the lab’s own post cannot rule out de-identified product usage. Enterprise and API default to no-train. Plus, Pro, and Free default to train-on unless the user finds Data Controls. There is no artifact the customer can take to a board — or to a coauthor — that says session S was excluded from every subsequent training mix.
**Paying today.** X is paying ChatGPT Enterprise and/or Codex via ChatGPT Business / Enterprise (no-train-by-default SKUs), sometimes next to GitHub Copilot Business on the same developers.
**Y today.** OpenAI’s Enterprise privacy page and the Codex security white paper say business inputs and outputs are not used for training unless an API org explicitly opts in; personal Plus/Pro workspaces train unless the user toggles “Improve the model for everyone” off. Retention and Compliance API logs exist for Enterprise. None of that is a customer-verifiable exclusion receipt tied to a session hash. GitHub Copilot Business similarly promises no training on Enterprise-managed seats and now streams session records to a SIEM — an audit of what happened, not a proof of what will not be in GPT-7. Closest threatening feature: the no-train contract plus opt-out. A contract is not a receipt. OpenAI’s NS post is the exhibit: policy language and “we cannot rule it out” can coexist.
**SaaS wedge.** A training-exclusion notary: client-side hash of each prompt/file sent to a lab or IDE agent, a signed attestation request the vendor must return (or the session does not proceed), and a quarterly bundle GC can hand an auditor. Start with Codex/ChatGPT and Copilot Business connectors. Charge per seat and per attested session.
**Conversion test.** If X is already paying ChatGPT Enterprise, they attach this on the privacy/compliance line rather than leave Enterprise. The unlock is that research and legal staff who refused to put unpublished work into Y will start using the paid seats once a receipt exists. They will not rip out Enterprise for a wrapper model.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Labs win when more professional work flows through the product. A cryptographic “this session is off-limits” button that can fail closed is a support load and a training-data hole they have no incentive to certify.
