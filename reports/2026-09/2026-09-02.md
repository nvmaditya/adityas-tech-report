# Aditya's Tech Report — 2 Sep 2026

Two labs split the same day into a cheaper coding model and a cyber-critical one, Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs shipped a camera-native world model, and YC’s data shop jumped an order of magnitude in five months.

## 1. Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.1; Mythos 5.1 stays behind a gate

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** The same weights now ship as a general coding model and as a restricted cyber/life-sciences model, with the bill cut on cache reads rather than list price.

Anthropic released Fable 5.1 as generally available and Mythos 5.1 only through trusted-access programs for defensive cybersecurity and life-sciences work. List price is unchanged at $10 / $50 per million input / output tokens; cache reads drop 75 percent to $0.25 per million, which Anthropic says cuts typical token-billed workloads about 25 percent and highly agentic ones up to about 45 percent. Fable 5.1 posts 55.8 percent on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 73.4 percent on CursorBench 3.2.0; Mythos 5.1 reaches 60.9 percent on the coding bench. Fable may discover vulnerabilities but not write exploits; those dual-use jobs stay on Opus or on Mythos under the Cyber Verification Program. Independent cost trackers already argue real invoices can still rise if the model spends more tokens than Fable 5 did.

## 2. OpenAI rates Astra Critical for cyber and will gate the exploit path

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** A frontier lab has now officially crossed its own highest cyber bar and is shipping the model anyway, with the dangerous slice reserved for a named defender program.

OpenAI said Tuesday that Astra meets the Critical cybersecurity threshold in its Preparedness Framework: with tools and access, it can find previously unknown flaws and develop exploits across many well-protected systems without step-by-step human guidance. It scored 100 percent on ExploitBench and, in an internal port of that bench, chained two zero-days the company says it is disclosing to maintainers. A public Astra is still promised “soon”; the advanced cyber tools start with alpha testers on Daybreak Blue (Cisco, Cloudflare, Palo Alto Networks among the named partners) and may pause or stop even legitimate defensive work. This is the first OpenAI model rated Critical in any Preparedness domain. The August training freeze is what this post is closing.

## 3. AfterQuery hits $3.2B and becomes YC’s fastest unicorn

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The scarce input is no longer another chatbot. It is paid expert traces of how a practitioner finishes a job.

Forbes and TechCrunch, citing people familiar and YC partner Gustaf Alströmer, put AfterQuery at a $3.2 billion valuation five months after a $30 million Series A at $300 million. The W25 pair — founder Spencer Mateega is 23 — sells human-curated reasoning data and RL environments rather than Q&A labels. Named customers include Nvidia’s Nemotron line, Thinking Machines Lab, Legora, and Korean lab Motif; the company also works with Chinese labs. April ARR was $100 million; a July post put recurring revenue in the “hundreds of millions.” AfterQuery declined to comment. Treat the $3.2 billion figure as sourced, not a filed round.

## 4. World Labs opens Atlas, a camera-native omni world model

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** Camera geometry is now a first-class input, not a sentence the video model has to guess.

Fei-Fei Li’s World Labs posted Atlas on 1 September: a multimodal autoregressive diffusion transformer pretrained from scratch on text, images, video, and 3D, with all inputs folded into a shared spatial context. Camera-controlled generation returns up to one minute of 1440p video; reconstruction emits frames plus explicit 3D (point clouds or Gaussian splats) from as few as two or three images. Company raters picked Atlas over MiniMax H3, Gemini Omni Flash, FLUX 3, and Seedance 2.5 in 75–94 percent of head-to-heads. Those numbers are first-party. Early access is request-only; Atlas is meant to power later Marble versions, not replace the November 2025 product overnight.

## 5. ChatGPT for Healthcare plugs into Epic, read-only

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The largest US EHR just became a context source for a general-purpose agent workspace, with write-back explicitly off.

OpenAI connected ChatGPT for Healthcare to Epic, which holds records for more than 325 million patients. Clinicians in approved HIPAA workspaces can pull authorized notes, labs, meds, and specialist docs into ChatGPT, or, in some deployments, run the assistant inside the chart. The company says the link is read-only: no orders, no chart edits, no override of existing permissions. A Healthcare Public Data plugin adds structured connectors to nine official sources including PubMed, DailyMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, RxNorm, and CMS Coverage. UCSF Health is the named pilot. Individual ChatGPT for Clinicians accounts do not get Epic.

Sources: [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-connects-health-records-and-healthcare-sources/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/01/chatgpt-health-adds-epic-integration-for-clinicians-to-import-patient-data/)

## 6. Manus is an independent agent lab again after Beijing unwound Meta’s deal

**Tag:** shift
**Why it matters.** A $2 billion agent acquisition lasted four months on paper and produced a data-deletion notice, not a Meta product line.

Manus said Tuesday it has formally resumed independent operations, with the founding team running it as an “independent agent lab.” Meta announced the purchase on 29 December 2025; China’s foreign-investment security reviewers prohibited the deal on 27 April 2026 and ordered it revoked. In August Manus told some users to back up data generated on or after the announcement date so it could erase that slice “to comply with regulatory requirements in specific parts of the world.” The company launched in March 2025 as a general agent for research and report writing, founded in China and later headquartered in Singapore. Cross-border agent M&A now has a working example of a forced unwind.

## 7. Apple says an OpenAI hire ran its schematic through an agent on LTspice

**Tag:** news
**Why it matters.** The trade-secret fight is no longer only about copied files. Apple is arguing that an agent that trained on the file keeps using the secret.

In a Monday filing Apple said forensic work on Chang Liu’s MacBook — handed over 21 August, six weeks after the July suit — showed he downloaded a confidential circuit schematic and used it at OpenAI. Messages from March describe an AI agent that learned to run LTspice and review the results. Apple also says Liu discussed “destroying the types of forensic data Apple needs” after learning of the internal investigation in June, and that OpenAI colleagues knew he still had access to Apple’s third-party cloud store. OpenAI’s same-day response called the dispute “a mess of Apple’s own making,” pointing at personal iCloud use and short offboarding windows. The claims are allegations in a live case.

## 8. Firefox on iOS gets a built-in ad blocker

**Tag:** launch
**Why it matters.** After yesterday’s Chrome Web Store MV2 purge, Mozilla shipped the iOS feature the App Store will not let an extension provide.

Mozilla turned on Ad Blocker for Firefox on iOS, using Apple’s WebKit Content Blocker API and EasyList. It is off by default under Settings → Browsing → Ad Blocker and can be toggled from the site menu. Third-party ads and ad-related trackers can be stopped before load; first-party ads, search ads, and Firefox’s own sponsored new-tab shortcuts stay. Brave, Vivaldi, and Opera already ship the same class of blocker. On iOS that is the ceiling: every browser is WebKit, and content blockers are an app feature, not an add-on store.

## Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

### 1. Agent file tools ingest secrets and there is no unlearn button

**Who.** IP counsel, hardware-security owner, or platform eng at a company whose coding or computer-use agents may open CAD, schematics, customer exports, or “the deck from last quarter.”
**What's broken.** Apple’s filing describes the scene: a confidential circuit schematic lands in LTspice, an agent is told to run the sim and read the output, and the secret is now in the session, the tool trace, and possibly the fine-tune or memory store. Shipping agents treat `open_file` / `upload` as convenience. They do not classify the file, bind it to a purpose, or prove it was dropped. Yesterday’s session-URL problem was about git metadata. This is the file itself becoming durable context.
**SaaS wedge.** An ingest gate in front of agent file and computer-use tools: MIME and schematic/CAD detectors, customer-owned allowlists, a purpose ticket that expires with the task, and a signed “this byte range never left the session” receipt. No write to memory, eval logs, or provider fine-tunes without an explicit second control.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Labs want the agent to “just open the file.” DLP products scan email and SaaS drives, not a headless agent’s tool argument. Once the weights or the memory store have seen the schematic, deletion is a support ticket the vendor will not honor.

Sources: [9to5Mac filing recap](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/31/apple-openai-forensic-macbook-evidence/) · [YC RFS — Software for Agents](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

### 2. Clinical agents summarize the chart and cannot point at the field

**Who.** CMIO, health-system AI lead, or compliance owner turning on ChatGPT-in-Epic or a rival EHR plugin.
**What's broken.** Today’s Epic connector is read-only and permission-preserving. That is necessary and not sufficient. The clinician still gets a fluent “what changed since last visit” with no click-through to the encounter, lab accession, or med list row that justified the sentence. When the summary is wrong, there is no eval pack that fails the build. OpenAI’s own help text still puts the burden on the human to “review the underlying record.” That does not scale past the UCSF pilot.
**SaaS wedge.** A grounded-summary sidecar for EHR agents: every claim is tied to a FHIR resource id and timestamp, a change-detection eval that must beat a held-out chart set, and a one-click “show the row” control inside the chart. Sell to the health system, not to the model lab.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Labs are paid to be in the workflow. EHRs are paid for seats and interfaces. Neither is paid when a summary quietly drops a potassium. Field-level provenance looks like latency and a lawsuit surface.

Sources: [OpenAI Healthcare](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-connects-health-records-and-healthcare-sources/) · [OpenAI Help — Epic plugin](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001490) · [YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)

### 3. Agent add-ons keep changing after the security review

**Who.** CISO or AI-platform owner who approved an MCP server, skill pack, or browser connector last quarter and now has hundreds of them.
**What's broken.** AIR came out of stealth today with $50 million from Sequoia and Greenoaks to sit inline in front of agent add-ons. Their scan found more than 17,800 public add-ons pointing at untrusted sources and brand-impersonation packs wearing Anthropic and OpenAI names. The review that let a skill in does not re-run when the pack’s scripts, allowed-tools, or upstream URL change. That is a different hole from yesterday’s static SKILL.md privilege audit: the artifact was clean at install and hostile at week six.
**SaaS wedge.** A continuous admission controller for MCP servers and skill packs: signed manifests, a diff of tools and network destinations against the last approved version, impersonation checks against lab trademarks, and an automatic quarantine when the hash moves. Charge per agent fleet, not per scan.
**Why incumbents leave it.** Marketplaces win on time-to-first-tool. Re-running the review on every push looks like friction. Endpoint vendors still inventory laptops, not the JSON the agent fetched this morning.
