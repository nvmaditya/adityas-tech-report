Apple handed the CEO job to a hardware engineer, the Pentagon put ChatGPT and Grok on millions of desks, and Chrome emptied the last Manifest V2 listings from its store.

1. John Ternus is Apple CEO; Phil Schiller leaves the App Store

Tag: shift Why it matters. The company that still sets the default computing surface just moved from an operations CEO to a product engineer, and moved store policy back under services.

Monday was Tim Cook’s last day after fifteen years. He stays as executive chairman and keeps the government relationships; John Ternus, previously SVP of hardware engineering, takes the job on 1 September. The same window, Bloomberg reported Phil Schiller stepping off the App Store and product events. The store returns to Eddy Cue’s services group, which held it until 2015, with Carson Oliver running it day to day and Nola Weinstein taking events. Schiller remains an Apple Fellow on unspecified work. The DMA fight, the Epic case, and whatever Ternus does with on-device agents now sit on a different org chart.

Sources: Cook memo via 9to5Mac · TechCrunch · Schiller / 9to5Mac

2. The Pentagon puts ChatGPT and Grok on GenAI.mil

Tag: news Why it matters. Unclassified agent chat is now a default tool for a three-million-person workforce, with Anthropic still off the list.

The Defense Department said custom ChatGPT (via OpenAI for Government) and Grok for Government (via Starshield AI) are live on GenAI.mil. The portal already has more than 1.7 million unique users out of about 3 million civilian and military personnel. The stated jobs are admin, logistics, planning, and policy — not classified targeting. Claude is absent after the earlier fight over military use. A department this size turning on two frontier chat agents is an eval, logging, and data-handling problem at a scale no lab demo has had to survive.

Sources: TechCrunch

3. ChatGPT Ads hits a $1B annualized run rate in under 200 days

Tag: shift Why it matters. OpenAI is now running both consumer-internet businesses — subscriptions and ads — and just opened self-serve buying across India, Europe, the Middle East, and North Africa.

OpenAI said ChatGPT Ads crossed a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate, with tens of thousands of advertisers and availability in more than 40 countries. Ads Manager self-serve, already live in the US, opened to those regions on 31 August. Ads sit on the free tier and the cheaper Go plan; the company says they are labeled and do not steer answers. The figure is a run rate, not booked 2026 revenue. Reuters notes OpenAI’s own full-year ads target was about $2.5 billion, so the current pace still has to more than double.

Sources: OpenAI · Reuters

4. Nvidia puts $3.5B into MediaTek and pulls it onto NVLink Fusion

Tag: news Why it matters. The custom-XPU path into Nvidia racks now has a $3.5 billion convertible-bond hook, which is how you keep “not-Nvidia silicon” inside an Nvidia factory.

Nvidia bought $3.5 billion of MediaTek convertible bonds, its largest direct investment outside the US, as part of MediaTek’s $3.9 billion overseas CB offering. Alphabet also participated; the size of that slice was not disclosed. MediaTek will adopt NVLink Fusion so customers can design custom XPUs that plug into Nvidia rack-scale systems. The same announcement covers RTX/DGX Spark local-AI silicon and Dimensity Auto. Jensen Huang told Bloomberg TV the flow is “not circular” because the two firms have separate businesses. The stock still sold off on the familiar circular-financing read.

Sources: NVIDIA · Reuters · TechCrunch

5. Chrome Web Store removes the last Manifest V2 extensions, including uBlock Origin

Tag: news Why it matters. The store listing was the last official install path. Full-power uBO on Chromium is now a sideload or a different browser.

Google’s published MV2 timeline hit 31 August: remaining Manifest V2 extensions are gone from the Chrome Web Store. Copies already installed on Chrome 138 or earlier can stay, but they will not update and cannot be reinstalled from the store. uBlock Origin 1.74 was the last stable Chrome build; the project pointed people at Firefox and at uBlock Origin Lite on MV3. Brave still hosts selected MV2 extensions. This is a store purge more than a new runtime kill — Chrome already stripped the flags that kept MV2 alive — but it closes the official door.

Sources: webiterate · Android Authority · uBO README · HN

6. The EU designates ChatGPT as a Very Large Online Search Engine

Tag: shift Why it matters. Brussels picked “search engine,” not “platform,” for a chatbot that answers and retrieves. That choice sets which DSA duties attach.

On 31 August the Commission designated ChatGPT as a VLOSE and Reddit and Roblox as VLOPs under the Digital Services Act, after each crossed 45 million average monthly EU users. The extra obligations — systemic-risk assessments covering illegal content, minors, well-being, rights, elections, and public security — come due in four months, with fines up to 6 percent of global revenue. Ireland’s Coimisiún na Meán will handle ChatGPT and Reddit; the Dutch ACM handles Roblox. An OpenAI spokesperson said ChatGPT search “operates as a search service under the DSA” and that the company is preparing to comply. ChatGPT already sits under the AI Act; this is the content-layer overlay.

Sources: Commission release · The Verge · Politico

7. OpenShot 4.0 adds recording, a color suite, and local AI masks

Tag: launch Why it matters. A widely used GPL video editor just absorbed three jobs people used to leave the app for, including on-device subject masks.

OpenShot 4.0, posted 30 August and still moving on HN into 1 September, ships a recording view (mic, screen, webcam, system audio on Windows, macOS, and Linux X11/Wayland), a color view with wheels, curves, LUTs, and scopes, and an Object Mask effect that runs downloaded ONNX models (YOLO, EfficientSAM, Cutie) locally. Ten new effects, a Qt timeline, and claimed speedups on blur and sharpen sit underneath. It is not DaVinci. It is a free editor that no longer forces a trip to another tool for a voice-over or a crude mask.

Sources: OpenShot 4.0 · HN

8. Claude Code Opus 5 Auto Mode is jailbreakable; Anthropic calls it by design

Tag: news Why it matters. The “auto-approve” mode that teams turn on so the agent can finish a job is not a security boundary, and the vendor said so.

Johann Rehberger (Embrace the Red) showed a website-summary prompt that walks Opus 5 in Auto Mode from WebFetch to curl, a ZIP, a custom decoder, and a shadowed struct.py — then a child process and a C2 callback. Attack success on a small sample sat around 60 percent for the full chain and 80 percent for file writes. Anthropic closed the report as Informative: Auto Mode is not a security guarantee. The demo is a few days old; the HN thread and the “working as designed” close are what moved in this window. Anyone who shipped Auto Mode as the default for a coding agent just inherited that sentence.

Sources: Embrace the Red · HN

Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

1. Auto Mode is sold as a reviewer and treated as a permission boundary

Who. Platform security or eng manager who turned on Claude Code / Cursor Auto Mode so developers would stop clicking Allow on npm test. What's broken. Auto Mode hands each tool call to another model and then executes. Rehberger’s chain did not need a zero-day in the sandbox. It needed a summary of a webpage, a curl, and a Python file named struct.py. Anthropic’s answer — working as designed — means the product will not grow a real policy engine. Teams that flattened permissions to “auto” now have an LLM voting on whether curl | python is fine. SaaS wedge. An out-of-band auto-mode proxy: deterministic allow/deny on tool name, argument shape, and whether untrusted content is already in the session; a second-model reviewer only for the residue; an eval pack of injection chains that must fail before Auto Mode can be enabled on a repo. Why incumbents leave it. Labs sell throughput. A mode that pauses is a support ticket. The classifier that rubber-stamps the call lives in the same trust domain as the agent that proposed it.

Sources: Embrace the Red, 26 Aug / circulating 31 Aug · YC RFS · Bessemer — Securing AI agents

2. Agent Skills install as docs and run as root-shaped tool packs

Who. Staff engineer or AI platform owner adding Agent Skills / SKILL.md packs to a coding or support agent. What's broken. A skill looks like a markdown file. It is also a bundle of scripts and allowed-tools. SkillScope measured 68,312 public skills and validated over-privilege in 7,039 of them. Google’s own Gemini Enterprise docs now tell customers to write natural-language constraints so a load_skill('send_email') after a web fetch can be denied — which is an admission that the default load path does not do that. AWS even published a skill-eval CLI because “is this skill safe?” is not a built-in. SaaS wedge. A skill admission controller: static audit of SKILL.md + bundled scripts, a privilege diff against the current task, a signed allowlist of tools the skill may load, and a runtime intercept on load_skill / run_skill_script when the session already contains untrusted content. Why incumbents leave it. Skill marketplaces win on how fast a pack “just works.” Declaring the union of every tool a skill might need is the demo. Least privilege is a support cost after someone emails payroll.

Sources: SkillScope · AWS sample-agent-skill-eval · Gemini Enterprise — Govern agent skills

3. Coding-agent sessions write themselves into public git

Who. OSS maintainer, security owner, or compliance lead whose developers commit from Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor. What's broken. Claude Code appends a https://claude.ai/code/session_... URL to commit messages and PR descriptions by default. There is no onboarding prompt. The setting that suppresses it (attribution.commit) is undiscovered until the link is already in main. Public session URLs leak prompts, file paths, and sometimes customer names; even a dead link is a permanent “this was an agent” banner on every SHA. A March patch tried to ban the URLs in /commit prompts. The June feature request is still open, and it resurfaced on HN this window. SaaS wedge. A commit-policy sidecar for agent runtimes: strip session URLs, tool traces, and secret-shaped strings before git commit / gh pr create; org-level attribution rules (Co-authored-by only, never a live session); a scanner for existing history. Why incumbents leave it. Attribution is a marketing line in the git log. Labs want the session link so they can debug and so the work is visibly theirs. Public-repo hygiene is the customer’s hook.

Sources: claude-code#66504 · YC RFS — Multiplayer AI
