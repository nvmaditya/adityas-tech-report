DeepSeek replaced its own flagship with a cheaper Flash model the same day Cognition put a Kimi-based SWE-2 inside Devin and Anthropic accused seven Chinese labs of distilling Claude at industrial scale.

1. DeepSeek ships V4.1-Flash and starts retiring V4-Pro

Tag: launch Why it matters. The cheap model is no longer the side SKU. It is the default, and the previous flagship is being routed into it.

On 10 September DeepSeek released DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, a 552-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with a new causal encoder–decoder that activates 8 billion parameters on prefill and 16 billion on decode. The company says the KV cache now needs a quarter of the previous generation’s HBM and an eighth of the SSD footprint, and that third-party tests put Flash ahead of DeepSeek-V4-Pro on quality, cost, speed, and wall-clock. The API name is deepseek-flash. V4-Flash and V4-Flash-Vision-Exp already route there; from 14 September 04:00 UTC, deepseek-v4-pro will too, at Flash rates, until a V4.1-Pro exists. Peak/off-peak pricing flipped the same morning. DeepSeek says it will work with the open-source community on inference. Treat the architecture claim as the product: agent sessions are cache-bound, and a lab that compresses that cache can undercut everyone else’s token bill.

Sources: DeepSeek · Reuters · SiliconANGLE · HN

2. Cognition launches SWE-2 inside Devin, free on paid plans for a month

Tag: launch Why it matters. The coding-agent company is no longer only a harness. It is selling a model it RL’d on someone else’s open base, at a price the frontier labs will not match.

Cognition’s 10 September post introduces SWE-2, post-trained from Moonshot’s 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3. On Cognition’s table it scores 50.0 percent on FrontierCode 1.1 Main — one point behind Claude Fable 5.1, a few behind GPT-6 Astra — and 92.8 percent on Terminal-Bench 2.1, the best row in that chart. Terminal-Bench 4 is the tell: 27.3 percent against 55.8 and 57.9 for Fable 5.1 and Astra. Cost claim: 64 percent cheaper than Fable 5.1 at the FrontierCode point, about a quarter of Astra. The new recipe trains medium, high, and max effort in one RL run with a Pareto cost penalty. SWE-2 is live in Devin Desktop and CLI, rolling to Web and Fusion, not as weights or a public API. Paid Devin plans get it free for a month. The product bet is that a specialized RL stack on a Chinese open base, locked to Devin’s harness, is enough to keep the $20–team seat from walking to Astra.

Sources: Cognition · Cognition on X · HN

3. Anthropic’s September threat report names distillation farms and weapons cells

Tag: news Why it matters. The lab that sells Claude as the safe enterprise model published an eight-month ledger of who actually used it, including other labs.

Anthropic’s 10 September Threat Intelligence report covers December 2025 through August 2026 across cyber, surveillance, influence, fraud, biological misuse, conventional weapons, and illicit distillation. The distillation chapter is the industry event: Anthropic says Alibaba’s Tongyi operation ran more than 151 million exchanges from May to July, peaking near 3 million a day across 3,500-plus fraudulent accounts, aimed at Qwen; Moonshot more than 23 million; DeepSeek 12.1 million in July; Zhipu, Xiaomi, SenseTime, and MiniMax smaller but the same shape. Separately it describes a Yemen-linked guided-rocket / ballistic / hypersonic cell, China-based anti-torpedo and electronic-warfare software work, and a Russia-linked drone-swarm effort, plus a suspected Midnight Blizzard mailbox-and-drone-SDK campaign. Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus carried almost all of it; Fable and Mythos appear only in one distillation case. Accounts were banned and classifiers tightened. The report is also a tariff: every cheap “Claude-compatible” proxy now has to answer whether it is a customer or a farm.

Sources: Anthropic · Reuters · SiliconANGLE · HN

4. OpenAI puts the Codex harness on an Agents API with hosted sandboxes

Tag: launch Why it matters. Codex is no longer only a product. It is the runtime other companies are being asked to rent.

On 10 September OpenAI opened a public-beta Agents API that runs the Codex harness in OpenAI’s cloud: sessions, context compaction, tool search, programmatic tool calling, and optional subagents. There is no harness surcharge — tokens and tools only. The environment is the fork. openai_hosted gives a Linux workspace with Python, Node, packages, setup commands, and Files-API inputs; env accepts string variables but rejects OPENAI_API_KEY and other reserved names. Partners on the first-class list: Blaxel, Cloudflare, Daytona, DigitalOcean, E2B, Modal, Oracle, Runloop, Vercel. Early design-partner numbers in the post: SafetyKit −60 percent cost per case, Hypha −86 percent failed responses, Ciridae eval 0.71 → 0.85 with a 4× latency cut. The beta header is OpenAI-Beta: agents=v1. The lock-in is not the model. It is the session object that already knows how Astra uses tools.

Sources: OpenAI · Agents API docs · Hosted sandboxes · HN

5. OpenAI pauses new ChatGPT Pro sign-ups because Astra is saturating the fleet

Tag: news Why it matters. The $200 tier is the overflow valve. Closing it is how a lab admits the model is more popular than the cluster.

Product lead Thibault Sottiaux said on 10 September that new ChatGPT Pro subscriptions are disabled. Pro “put the most strain on our systems”; Plus, Go, Enterprise, Business, and the API stay open; existing Pro seats are untouched. He had warned a day earlier that Astra demand was “unprecedented” and that a Pro pause was on the table. The company has not given a reopen date or a queue length. Astra launched 3 September and is still mid-rollout. A capacity freeze on the highest consumer SKU, one week in, is the opposite of the “AGI era” launch copy. Watch whether API rate limits or Codex pooled credits move next; those are the seats enterprises actually budget.

Sources: TechCrunch · The Verge

6. A second mathematician says OpenAI still has not shown its training trail

Tag: news Why it matters. The Navier–Stokes fight is no longer one professor. It is a class of people who typed unpublished work into the product and cannot audit the mix.

Andreas Thom, a TU Dresden geometer, posted on 10 September that one of OpenAI’s August results — non-sofic groups — sits on work he and Gábor Kun published, that he had discussed related methods in ChatGPT before the announcement, and that Mark Sellke’s reply (“that did not happen”) answered only whether researchers opened his threads, not whether de-identified product logs entered training. He called the answer dishonest. OpenAI had already amended the write-up after credit complaints and, on Navier–Stokes, written that it “cannot rule out” de-identified usage. A parallel HN thread the same day documented the consumer “allow training” toggle flipping back on after users turned it off. Enterprise and API default to no-train. Plus, Pro, and Free do not. Until there is a session-level receipt, every unpublished proof typed into Codex is a priority dispute waiting for a press cycle.

Sources: The Verge · Thom on Mathstodon · HN · Tell HN — training toggle

7. GPT-Live-1 lands in the API at five cents a minute

Tag: launch Why it matters. Full-duplex voice is now a metered layer other agents can speak through, not a ChatGPT-only demo.

OpenAI put GPT-Live-1 on the API on 10 September at $0.05 per minute for the voice front end, billed per second; the backend model and tools are extra. The pitch is one model listening and talking at once, instead of an STT–LLM–TTS chain. OpenAI’s own Full Duplex Bench number is +30 points versus GPT-Realtime-2.1; Speak reported ~80 percent fewer interruptions in a tutor eval; Yelp is using it for Host reservations. Twelve new voices, telephony support, and a pattern where Live-1 hands hard work to Astra or Codex mid-call. Concurrent-session caps scale with API tier (25 on Tier 1, 500 on Tier 5). Five cents a minute is $3 an hour before the reasoning model speaks. That is cheap against a human agent and expensive against a turn-based bot. The new failure mode is a tool call that fires while the caller is still saying no.

Sources: OpenAI · API model card · The Register

8. Universal Music and ElevenLabs announce a licensed fan-remix platform

Tag: launch Why it matters. A major label is no longer only suing generators. It is standing up a product next to them.

On 10 September UMG and ElevenLabs announced a multi-year licensing and product deal. The first artifact is an unnamed ElevenLabs-built platform, still in development, where fans remix, mash up, and “interpret” tracks from artists who opt in, plus “personalized vocal experiences.” It is separate from ElevenMusic and from ElevenLabs’ Music API. Artists choose whether to participate; the press copy promises they are paid. The timing is the point: Suno had just put out v6 as its first “in partnership with the music industry” suite with Warner, BMG, and Believe. UMG spent years in court over training data. This deal does not settle those cases. It does create a licensed sandbox a rights holder can point to when the next unlicensed remix hits a chart.

Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

1. The hosted sandbox still takes the secret as an environment variable

Who. CISO or VP Platform at a company wiring production agents through OpenAI’s new Agents API (or a partner sandbox) so the agent can call Stripe, GitHub, and the internal billing API. Budget sits with the platform lead who already signs the sandbox and API invoice. What's broken. The 10 September Agents API path of least resistance is environment.type = "openai_hosted" plus an env map. Docs allow arbitrary string env vars in the guest; they only reject reserved names such as OPENAI_API_KEY. The agent that dumps STRIPE_KEY into that map has just written a live credential onto a disk OpenAI provisions. A leaked session log, a printenv in a stack trace, or a package the agent just pip-installed can lift it. E2B’s current Enterprise page already advertises the opposite design — values resolve at egress, “no API response, log, or sandbox holds them” — but the default Agents API example does not use it. Paying today. X is paying OpenAI API tokens plus OpenAI-hosted containers (the Agents API sandbox SKU) and often an E2B Pro plan ($150/month + per-second compute) or Daytona/Blaxel as the “we might switch” line. Y today. OpenAI-hosted sandboxes are a managed Linux workspace: packages, setup commands, Files API inputs, network templates that a session cannot broaden. Closest threatening feature: reject-list on reserved env names, plus the partner slot for E2B. E2B Enterprise already runs a Firecracker microVM per session, per-sandbox egress allow/deny, and egress-resolved secrets that never enter the guest. What neither SKU ships as a portable control is a customer-signed secret broker that every harness — OpenAI-hosted, E2B, Daytona — must call: the tool request leaves the sandbox with a handle, the broker injects the header on the allowed host, and the value is never in /proc, the env, or the session transcript. OpenAI is not going to make “your key never touches our VM” the default while the quickstart still shows env. SaaS wedge. An egress secret broker with a short SDK: the agent receives only handles; the broker binds handle → host → header, logs the bind, and refuses unknown destinations. Seat plus per-injected call. First design partner is anyone already on Agents API openai_hosted. Conversion test. If X is already paying OpenAI containers and/or E2B Pro, they attach this on the same sandbox-plus-compliance line rather than rip out either vendor, because Y’s job is “give the agent a machine” and this job is “prove the machine never held the key.” Unlock motion: more Agents API sessions get approved once finance can see Stripe keys are not in the guest. Why incumbents leave it. OpenAI is selling time-to-first-agent. Putting secrets in env is what every quickstart already does. E2B can differentiate on its own cloud; it cannot force OpenAI’s hosted path to stop accepting env vars without losing the partner listing.

Sources: OpenAI hosted sandboxes · Agents API intro · E2B Enterprise secrets · YC RFS — Multiplayer AI

2. Full-duplex voice can execute a tool before the caller finishes saying no

Who. VP Support or Contact Center owner at a company putting a voice agent on the same number that already runs Amazon Connect or a Twilio trunk. Budget sits with the CX lead who already defends the per-minute Connect / Q invoice. What's broken. GPT-Live-1’s 10 September API pitch is that one model listens and talks at once. That is the product. It is also the incident: the caller says “wait, not that card” while the agent has already issued refund.create. Turn-based IVR at least had a “press 1 to confirm.” Full-duplex plus tool-calling has no frozen confirmation object. Yelp Host and Speak’s tutor evals measure interruptions and latency, not whether a spoken veto arrived in time to cancel the side effect. Paying today. X is paying Amazon Connect voice ($0.038/min on the current bundled SKU in many regions) plus Amazon Q in Connect self-service ($0.008/min voice when Q is on the contact) or a Twilio ConversationRelay + model bill, often next to an OpenAI Live-1 front end at $0.05/min. Y today. Amazon Q in Connect is sold as agent-assist recommendations and as a self-service bot on the same contact. Contact Lens already stores the transcript and can fire Contact Lens rules. Connect Flows plus Lambda are how a “refund” actually happens. Closest threatening feature: a Flow that pauses for a DTMF or a Lex confirmation slot before invoking Lambda. That pause is turn-based. It does not hash the last two seconds of audio to the exact JSON the tool is about to send, and it does not abort an in-flight Live-1 tool call when the caller talks over it. Twilio ConversationRelay streams audio to your model; it does not bind utterance ↔ payload either. Amazon is not going to ship “block our own Q tool call because the customer was still talking” — that metric makes Q look slower on the demo. SaaS wedge. A spoken-action bind sitting between the voice model and the tool gateway: buffer the tool request, play or display a one-line confirmation that names amount and destination, accept only a matching short utterance or DTMF, hash audio+payload, and drop anything that diverges. Per-minute attach on the same Connect/Twilio trunk. Conversion test. If X is already paying Connect + Q, they attach this on the telephony-plus-compliance line rather than rip out Connect, because the last overlapping-speech refund is already a chargeback and Q’s job is “talk to the customer,” not “prove the charge matched the last completed sentence.” Unlock motion: more Live-1 minutes get approved once tool calls cannot race the caller. Why incumbents leave it. Connect and Live-1 are sold on containment rate and natural turn-taking. A product whose success metric is “we waited” taxes both SKUs.

Sources: GPT-Live-1 · Amazon Connect pricing — Q in Connect · YC RFS — Multiplayer AI

3. The AI gateway cannot prove the upstream is the lab and not a distillation farm

Who. CISO or Head of AI Platform at a company that routes Claude / GPT traffic through a gateway so finance can see tokens, and that has started allowing “Claude-compatible” cheaper endpoints for overflow. Budget sits with the platform owner who already pays the gateway and the model invoice. What's broken. Anthropic’s 10 September report is the scene: Moonshot allegedly relayed hundreds of thousands of live customer chats into Claude; Alibaba 151 million exchanges; DeepSeek 12.1 million. From the buyer’s side the request still looks like an OpenAI-shaped POST. Cloudflare AI Gateway and Portkey will happily log it. They will not tell the CISO that the base_url they allowlisted is a reseller replaying chain-of-thought into someone else’s training mix, or that two tenants are sharing a session cookie on the other side. Paying today. X is paying Cloudflare AI Gateway (Workers Paid / unified Workers AI + Gateway control plane) or Portkey / Helicone on top of Anthropic Claude for Work or an Azure OpenAI / Bedrock spend line. Y today. Cloudflare AI Gateway now sits on the same control plane as Workers AI. It logs prompts and completions by default, supports per-request cf-aig-collect-log / cf-aig-collect-log-payload overrides, DLP-scans tool-call arguments in the JSON body, and can export OTEL spans with gen_ai.prompt_json. Closest threatening feature: turn payload logging off and turn DLP on. That stops your secrets hitting Cloudflare’s log. It does not attest that the TLS name you think is api.anthropic.com is Anthropic, that the account on the other side is yours, or that the response CoT is not being stored by a third-party relay. Cloudflare’s job is a proxy you control. It is not a lab-identity receipt. Anthropic will keep banning farms from its side; it will not give every enterprise a signed “this response never left our rack” header, because that header would also prove when it did. SaaS wedge. An upstream-attestation layer in front of the gateway: pin lab endpoints by cert and account, refuse lookalike OpenAI-compatible hosts unless they present a lab-issued tenant token, watermark outgoing CoT so a replay shows up in the lab’s own TI sweep, and emit a signed per-session receipt the CISO can put next to the Anthropic invoice. Seat plus per-attested million tokens. Conversion test. If X is already paying Cloudflare AI Gateway plus Claude, they attach this rather than rip out the gateway, because Gateway’s job is “route and log” and this job is “prove we were not last month’s 3 million-requests-a-day row.” Unlock motion: overflow to third-party compatible endpoints stays blocked until the receipt exists. Why incumbents leave it. Cloudflare is paid on requests through the proxy. A product that fails closed when the upstream looks cheap shrinks Gateway volume. Anthropic’s incentive is to write the next TI report, not to instrument every customer’s gateway.

Sources: Anthropic TI — September 2026 · Reuters distillation claims · Cloudflare AI Gateway logging · Cloudflare AI Gateway DLP
