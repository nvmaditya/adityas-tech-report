11:T5910,<h2>1. ChatGPT&#39;s ad collector binds other websites to the logged-in account</h2>
<p><strong>Tag:</strong> news
<strong>Why it matters.</strong> The public ads pitch was aggregate clicks. The cookie on <code>.openai.com</code> is an account handle.</p>
<p>Buchodi published the capture on 20 September. On <code>chatgpt.com</code> the client mints 16 random bytes, exchanges them at <code>/backend-api/bazaar/obi/sync-token</code> for an RS256 JWT, and posts that token to <code>bzr.openai.com</code>, which sets <code>__obi</code> with <code>Domain=.openai.com; SameSite=None; Secure; Max-Age=31536000</code>. Advertiser pages that load <code>oaiq.min.js</code> from <code>bzrcdn.openai.com</code> send that cookie back with page URLs, form fields, and SHA-256 hashes of email and phone. One phone reproduced the path on 12 merchant sites, including Chewy, Wayfair, HelloFresh and Coursera, across 936 pixels and 1,029 hostnames in the broader log. OpenAI&#39;s own measurement-pixel docs describe first-party <code>__oppref</code> / <code>__obref</code> cookies and hashed advanced matching; they do not describe an account-scoped <code>__obi</code> on <code>.openai.com</code>. Ads stay off Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu. The collector does not.</p>

<h2>2. Google open-sources AX, a declarative orchestrator for agent fleets</h2>
<p><strong>Tag:</strong> launch
<strong>Why it matters.</strong> The labs have been running this internally. The primitives — Task, Workspace, Gateway, Model — are now a public control plane.</p>
<p><code>google/ax</code> landed as an official repo under the Google org and cut v0.3.0 on 20 September. AX declares isolated tasks with CPU and memory limits, wires a workspace from git repos, MCP servers or a plain-English goal, fences outbound traffic through a Gateway allowlist, and pins model secrets as cluster objects. It runs on Agent Substrate and is sold as the layer that can suspend and resume cheap actors instead of treating a coding agent as a microservice or a batch job. <code>ax ssh</code> is the debug path. This is not a new Gemini checkpoint. It is Google publishing the harness its own agent workloads already assume, the same week the HN thread on &quot;why MCP was always a bad idea&quot; was arguing the protocol is the wrong isolation boundary.</p>

<h2>3. The FT puts about $300 billion of AI infrastructure guarantees off the balance sheet</h2>
<p><strong>Tag:</strong> shift
<strong>Why it matters.</strong> The capex race is no longer just cash and purchase commitments. Residual-value promises now sit in the footnotes.</p>
<p>A Financial Times investigation dated 20 September, carried through secondary write-ups the same day, said Meta, Nvidia, Broadcom and peers have written up to about $300 billion of residual-value and lease guarantees over the past year so special-purpose vehicles can hold the debt. Alphabet&#39;s data-centre lease guarantees are reported up from $16.9 billion to $43.8 billion in six months, with under 2 percent booked as a liability — $815 million of estimated probable loss against that $43.8 billion ceiling. Nvidia&#39;s reported ceiling tied to a SoftBank / OpenAI campus is $105 billion; Broadcom&#39;s chip-lease exposure around Anthropic is about $29 billion. Separate from that $300 billion guarantee stack, the same desks keep a ~$3 trillion figure for unstarted leases and purchase commitments. Those are different columns. Both live outside the headline leverage ratio.</p>

<h2>4. Qwen Image 2.1 unifies generation and editing, including native RGBA</h2>
<p><strong>Tag:</strong> launch
<strong>Why it matters.</strong> Product shots, infographics and storyboards are now one 7B visual stack with ten reference images, not a generator plus a separate layer model.</p>
<p>Alibaba&#39;s Qwen team posted Qwen-Image-2.1 on 20 September. The visual generation trunk is a 7B mixed-granularity attention stack (32 single-stream DiT layers) with KV-cache reuse. <h2>Buildable pain points</h2>
<p>Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.</p>
<h3>1. The ChatGPT Go account used for work still wears the ads collector on every merchant site</h3>
<p><strong>Who.</strong> CISO or Head of IT at a 200–5,000-person company that bought ChatGPT Business or Enterprise for a slice of staff and left everyone else on Free or Go. Budget sits with the person who already signs the Enterprise invoice and the CASB / SSE seat (Zscaler, Netskope, or Cloudflare Gateway).
<strong>What&#39;s broken.</strong> Buchodi&#39;s 20 September capture is the scene: a logged-in ChatGPT session mints <code>__obi</code> on <code>.openai.com</code> with <code>SameSite=None</code>, and the OAIQ SDK on Chewy, Wayfair or Coursera sends that handle back with the page the employee just used to price a vendor. Official ads docs still say Plus / Pro / Business / Enterprise / Edu have no ads and that advertisers see aggregates. They do not say a Go account used at lunch stops joining off-site events to the same <code>sub</code> that later pastes a customer contract into ChatGPT.
<strong>Paying today.</strong> X is paying ChatGPT Business or Enterprise (no-ads SKU) for a subset of seats, plus Zscaler Internet Access or Cloudflare Gateway as the corporate web proxy.
<strong>Y today.</strong> ChatGPT Enterprise was bought as the no-training, no-ads workspace with SSO and a retention policy. Help-center copy is explicit: ads are a Free / Go surface. Closest threatening feature: &quot;upgrade the remaining seats.&quot; That closes the in-product ad unit. It does not inspect <code>bzr.openai.com</code> / <code>bzrcdn.openai.com</code> event posts from merchant pages, and it does not emit a per-employee receipt that <code>__obi</code> was minted on a managed browser. Zscaler ZIA will block a listed domain if someone writes the policy. It does not know that the JWT in the cookie is the same account that is supposed to live only inside the Enterprise workspace. OpenAI will keep adding advertiser measurement. It will not ship &quot;our own Go pixel should have been dark on a corporate IdP session.&quot;
<strong>SaaS wedge.</strong> A ChatGPT-identity CASB control: detect <code>obi</code> / <code>oppref</code> minting on managed browsers, bind it to the IdP user, block <code>bzr.openai.com</code> event posts unless the account is a documented Enterprise workspace, and write a signed &quot;this seat is leaking&quot; ticket onto the existing SSE line.
<strong>Conversion test.</strong> If X is already paying ChatGPT Enterprise for 400 seats and leaving 1,200 on Go to save the subscription delta, they attach this and then convert the leaky Go seats, because Y&#39;s job is &quot;the work chat is not an ad surface&quot; and this job is &quot;the same account cannot be an ad identifier on Wayfair.&quot; Unlock motion: the remaining Go seats become an Enterprise line once a pixel event is a compliance finding, not a blog post.
<strong>Why incumbents leave it.</strong> OpenAI&#39;s ads business meters Free and Go. Zscaler&#39;s success metric is &quot;we proxied the request,&quot; not &quot;we proved this cookie is the Enterprise <code>sub</code>.&quot; A control whose success metric is &quot;we refused the measurement post&quot; makes both dashboards look worse.</p>

<h3>2. The cloud coding agent&#39;s allowlist still lets the vendor S3 bucket, and Zscaler never sees the hop</h3>
<p><strong>Who.</strong> CISO or VP Platform Engineering at a 150–2,000-engineer company that turned on Cursor Cloud Agents or GitHub Copilot cloud agents. Budget sits with the person who already signs Cursor Enterprise / Copilot Business and Zscaler ZIA or Cloudflare Gateway.
<strong>What&#39;s broken.</strong> a16z&#39;s still-circulating computer-use thesis is the brief; today&#39;s AX Gateway primitive is the tell. Cursor&#39;s own security-network doc is the scene: Cloud Agents default to open internet, &quot;Allowlist only&quot; still leaves Cursor services, SCM hosts, and <code>cloud-agent-artifacts.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com</code> reachable, and the published egress IPs are what <em>your</em> firewall is asked to trust — the opposite direction from a corporate proxy. A prompt-injected agent that &quot;uploads a demo&quot; has a blessed bucket. Zscaler saw none of the packets. They never left Cursor&#39;s VM.
<strong>Paying today.</strong> X is paying Cursor Enterprise (Team Premium / Cloud Agents on the same bill) or GitHub Copilot Business / Enterprise, plus Zscaler Internet Access or Cloudflare Gateway.
<strong>Y today.</strong> Cursor Enterprise was bought as the AI IDE. Cloud Agent network modes are real: Allow all, Default + allowlist, Allowlist only, lockable org-wide. Secrets, OIDC federation, Privacy Mode, and a GitHub egress proxy exist. Closest threatening feature: org-locked allowlist-only. That is a domain list the admin typed. It is not a per-task policy compiled from the ticket (&quot;this agent may hit <code>npmjs.com</code> and <code>api.github.com</code> for 20 minutes&quot;), it does not deny the artifact bucket without breaking PR demos, and it does not write a signed &quot;blocked host X&quot; event into the SIEM Zscaler already owns. Zscaler ZIA was bought as the web proxy for laptops. Cloud Agents do not send their tool traffic through it. Cursor will keep adding domains to the default list. It will not ship &quot;our own S3 demo bucket should have been a denied exfil.&quot;
<strong>SaaS wedge.</strong> A cross-vendor agent-egress broker that sits in front of Cursor Cloud, Copilot cloud agents, Claude Code cloud, and AX Gateway: compile a per-task allowlist from the ticket and IdP group, force artifact uploads through a customer-owned bucket, and emit deny receipts onto the existing Zscaler / Cloudflare line.
<strong>Conversion test.</strong> If X is already paying Cursor Enterprise and Zscaler ZIA, they attach this rather than rip Cloud Agents out, because Y&#39;s job is &quot;the agent can reach npm&quot; and this job is &quot;the agent cannot reach an undocumented host, including the vendor&#39;s own artifact bucket, without a ticket.&quot; Unlock motion: more repos come off the Cloud Agent blocklist once a denied hop is a SIEM row, not an incident channel.
<strong>Why incumbents leave it.</strong> Open internet is how Cloud Agents pass CI on the first try. Zscaler cannot inspect a VM it does not terminate. A control whose success metric is &quot;we refused the S3 PUT&quot; makes time-to-green look worse in the same adoption review.</p>

<h3>3. AI Studio says the prototype chat is gone; the backend session is still the same URL</h3>
<p><strong>Who.</strong> DPO or CISO at a company whose engineers prototype customer workflows in Google AI Studio or the Gemini Developer API before anyone has bought Vertex. Budget sits with the person who already signs Google Workspace (Gemini + Vault) and is being asked to approve Vertex / Gemini Enterprise for production.
<strong>What&#39;s broken.</strong> The 19–20 September reproduction is the scene: Permanent Delete removes the Drive shortcut, the UI says the item cannot be recovered, and restoring the JSON file resumes the identical session. Workspace Vault docs updated 19 September are explicit that admin retention can outlive a user delete on the Gemini <em>app</em>. AI Studio is the other product. Gemini Developer API ZDR docs say abuse logs and Search-grounding storage still exist unless the workload moves to Vertex. Legal thought GDPR Article 17 fired when the engineer hit delete.
<strong>Paying today.</strong> X is paying Google Workspace Business / Enterprise with Gemini and Vault, and is on a Vertex or Gemini Enterprise quote they have not yet signed because &quot;we&#39;re only prototyping.&quot;
<strong>Y today.</strong> Workspace Gemini was bought as the assistant inside Gmail and Docs, with Vault as the eDiscovery hold. Admin controls now let IT disable user-level temporary chat and deletion; Vault retention overrides those deletes on purpose. Vertex AI is the SKU Google points at when a customer asks for zero data retention. Closest threatening feature: &quot;turn the prototype off and use Vertex ZDR.&quot; That is a migration, not an attestation. It does not give the DPO a signed before/after on the specific AI Studio session URL the engineer already deleted, and it does not catch the next intern who pastes a customer dump into aistudio.google.com because the Vertex project is still in procurement. Google will keep improving Vault on the Gemini app. It will not ship &quot;our own AI Studio delete button should have been a verified backend purge with a customer-held receipt.&quot;
<strong>SaaS wedge.</strong> A Gemini-session deletion attestor: wrap AI Studio / Gemini Developer API projects used for prototypes, record the session id at create, on delete re-query the backend URL and the Drive shortcut, and fail the ticket closed until both 404. Per-project plus a share of the Vertex migration, attached to the Workspace / Vault line.
<strong>Conversion test.</strong> If X is already paying Workspace Gemini and Vault, they attach this and then unlock the Vertex ZDR spend, because Y&#39;s job is &quot;Gmail drafts are holdable&quot; and this job is &quot;the prototype that contained last week&#39;s customer file is actually gone.&quot; The attach sits on the privacy line they already defend; the unlock is the Vertex SKU they would not sign while AI Studio delete was a shrug.
