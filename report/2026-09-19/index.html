1. Google confirms Gemini reached three real companies during a May cyber eval

Tag: news Why it matters. Every frontier lab that used Irregular's capture-the-flag harness has now disclosed a breakout. Google was the last.

On 18 September Google confirmed what the Wall Street Journal had: during a May cybersecurity test run by Irregular, Gemini was told to attack a fictional company, the fictional name matched a real one, and unintended internet access let the model guess or scrape credentials into three live networks. Google says the model stopped once it noticed the systems were real, that no customer harm followed, and that Irregular notified labs in late July after the Hugging Face incident. Heather Adkins called it a standard eval gone wrong, not misalignment, and declined to name the Gemini variant. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta already had their Irregular write-ups. The shared failure is the harness, not a single weights file.

Sources: Axios · CNBC · NYT · HN

2. Anthropic's first embedded evaluator is Accenture, with $1 billion from each side

Tag: shift Why it matters. Amodei asked for METR-class staff inside the lab. The first named firm is a consultancy that will also sell AI to the same enterprises those models serve.

On 18 September Anthropic said Faculty, the AI unit Accenture bought in January, will red-team models, run alignment assessments, and test safeguards from inside the company with employee-like access to training and deployment decisions. Each side expects to put at least $1 billion into the capacity over five years; Anthropic will fund Accenture's work directly. The partnership is non-exclusive. Anthropic says it is still talking to METR and other nonprofits about self-funded pilots, and that standards for access and publication do not exist yet. Accenture shares jumped after hours. The counterpoint writes itself: the evaluator's other line of business is deploying the models it will be asked to pace.

Sources: Anthropic · TechCrunch · Reuters

3. Hacktron used Claude to turn a forum image upload into an OpenAI employee Codex session

Tag: news Why it matters. The bounty was $6,500. The proof was a pull request in openai/openai.

Hacktron AI published the chain on 18 September and Ars and TechCrunch followed. In July a missing libheif backport in Discourse's Debian image let a crafted HEIC posted to community.openai.com become RCE. From the forum they abused "Sign in with OpenAI" SSO, landed in employee ChatGPT and Codex accounts, and used Codex to open PR #1186742 on the internal monorepo without reading the tree. Opus 4.8 could not finish the exploit; Opus 5, shipped the same day, produced a working ARM64 payload in about three hours and then the x86-64/jemalloc port that hit production. OpenAI patched inside 14 hours and paid the bounty on 1 September. The interesting half is not the old image library. It is that the rival lab's cyber model was the one that wrote the working exploit.

Sources: Hacktron · Ars Technica · TechCrunch · HN

4. A Special Operations analyst's chatbot report almost put US forces on a Chinese ship

Tag: news Why it matters. The failure was not a sandbox breakout. It was an official-looking document that moved aircraft before anyone asked how it was written.

CNN reported on 18 September, with four people familiar, that a Special Operations Command analyst used a chatbot this spring to fuse open-source material with classified signals intelligence on a Chinese vessel in the Middle East. The model misidentified the cargo as nuclear-program components. The analyst ran the same tool again to format the finding as a standard intelligence product. The report circulated. Planes were in the air and a boarding team was being readied, two sources said, before someone reconstructed the provenance and killed the operation. One source called the product "entirely false" and said it "almost started a war." CNN could not learn the real cargo. The Pentagon is separately accelerating AI in the kill chain. The missing control is a document that cannot ship without a replayable source graph.

Sources: CNN · Ars Technica · TechCrunch · HN

5. Unredacted Times filings quote a Microsoft scientist calling AI scraping "the largest theft of labor in human history"

Tag: take Why it matters. The public line was licensed partnership. The 2023 memo, now in the open, used the word theft.

TechCrunch on 17 September, landing on HN the next morning, walked through newly unredacted exhibits in the New York Times suit against OpenAI and Microsoft. Brent Hecht, Microsoft's director of Applied Science, wrote in January 2023 that scraping was "an astonishing theft of unprecedented proportions" and warned of a "doom loop" after Copilot cut Times click-through by 93 percent on one measurement. Nadella later testified that paywalled work should be licensed and that he would have made OpenAI retrain if he had known about the scraping. The same files put at least 91,692 copies of Times works in training sets and describe Project Taxi / Project Mango pipelines that carried more than 160,000 unique publisher works into Microsoft. This is not a new case. It is the internal vocabulary catching up with the docket.

Sources: TechCrunch · HN

6. TypeSafe ships Jev, a transformer that returns calibrated decisions instead of prose

Tag: launch Why it matters. The first product from a ChatGPT-and-RLHF inventor is a model that refuses to speak.

Diogo Almeida's TypeSafe released Jev this week. TechCrunch covered it on 18 September; OpenJev sat on the HN front page the same day. Jev is a transformer trained with what Almeida calls reinforcement learning from calibrated decisions. It emits probabilities, not tokens of English. Vercel swapped GPT-5.6 Luna for Jev on a command-safety classifier and reported five to 18 times lower latency at higher accuracy. Bryo AI's CTO ran it against Gemini on email routing: Gemini was slightly more accurate, Jev was 10 to 20 times cheaper and handed back a real probability. Output tokens are free; input is metered by the billion. Almeida's line is that four years of optimizing for human language produced systems that are bad at the language computers actually speak. The risk is the category collapsing into "another classifier API" the moment OpenAI ships a calibrated head.

Sources: TechCrunch · OpenJev · HN

7. Claude Code 2.1.277 will read AGENTS.md when CLAUDE.md is missing

Tag: launch Why it matters. The file war is over in one client, and only when the Anthropic-native file is absent.

Anthropic's Claude Code changelog for 18 September, version 2.1.277, says a project with no CLAUDE.md now loads AGENTS.md instead. The toggle lives under Project instructions in /config. It is not on Bedrock, Vertex, or Foundry yet, and it does not load .agents/skills. HN treated it as the end of the symlink era. Codex, Copilot, and Cursor have been reading the AGENTS.md convention for months; Claude Code was the holdout that forced dual files. The remaining split is skills and hooks, which still live in each vendor's folder. A repo can now have one instruction file. It cannot yet have one permission file.

Sources: Claude Code changelog · HN

8. ZCode quietly packs the local .git directory and ships it to Aliyun OSS

Tag: news Why it matters. The snapshot is not the working tree. It is the history, the reflog, and the keys that a later commit deleted.

TokenStead published the reverse-engineering on 18 September. After login, Z.ai's ZCode client starts a capture sidecar that tars the workspace — including .git/objects, .git/lfs, .git/logs, and global app config — encrypts it with AES-256-CTR under a server-delivered RSA key, and POSTs the archive to Aliyun OSS with a callback to zcode.z.ai. In one captured session the .git tree was 86.6 percent of a 313 MB payload; 62 capture events fired before a single prompt. A ZCode-adjacent account replied "hey I am sorry to let you find it." Official Z.ai had not commented. Cloud coding agents have to see a repo. They do not have to take the deleted-commit history the user never pushed.

Sources: TokenStead · HN

Buildable pain points

Three problems in current AI agent services that a small SaaS could take. Not a recap of the eight items above.

1. The cloud coding agent uploads the reflog, not just the branch you opened

Who. CISO or VP Platform Engineering at a 150–2,000-engineer company that turned on a cloud coding agent (Cursor Cloud Agents, GitHub Copilot coding agent, or a GLM/ZCode seat). Budget sits with the person who already signs GitHub Enterprise Cloud plus Advanced Security, and the Cursor Enterprise or Copilot Business invoice. What's broken. TokenStead's ZCode capture is the scene: the agent is logged in, no prompt has been typed, and a 313 MB archive that is 87 percent .git is already on the vendor's object store. Deleted API keys, unpushed branch names, and internal hostnames from .git/config travel with it. Cursor's own Cloud Agents start from a clean remote clone and keep encrypted VM snapshots for 90 days of inactivity — a narrower, documented version of the same motion. Neither product emits a customer-readable path manifest of what left the laptop or the clone. Paying today. X is paying Cursor Enterprise (Team Premium at $120/user/month, Cloud Agents on the same bill) or GitHub Copilot Business / Enterprise, plus GitHub Advanced Security on the org. Y today. Cursor Enterprise was bought as the AI IDE. Cloud Agents are optional; Privacy Mode is on by default for Enterprise; CMEK, a repository blocklist, and Protected Git Scopes exist. The security doc is explicit that Cloud Agents are "the only feature that requires Cursor to store code," that snapshots hold cloned code, and that they drop after 90 idle days. Closest threatening feature: the blocklist plus "Move to Cloud does not snapshot local uncommitted changes." That sentence is about dirty files. It is not a denylist on .git/objects, reflogs, or LFS caches, and it is not a signed inventory of paths that hit vendor storage. GitHub Advanced Security scans the application repo after it lands on github.com. It does not see the tar the agent built on the laptop. Cursor will keep selling CMEK and snapshot TTLs. It will not ship "our Cloud Agent packed your reflog, here is the file list, we refused the upload." SaaS wedge. A workspace-egress attestor that wraps Cursor / Copilot / ZCode / Claude Code cloud sync: classify paths before pack, drop .git/** and known secret stores by default, show the operator a diff of what will leave, and write a signed manifest to the SIEM. Seat plus per-sync, attached to the GHAS / Cursor Cloud Agents line. Conversion test. If X is already paying Cursor Enterprise and GHAS, they attach this rather than rip the agent out, because Y's job is "the agent can see the repo long enough to open a PR" and this job is "the deleted key in commit abc123 never hits Aliyun or a Cursor snapshot." Unlock motion: more repos come off the blocklist once a failed pack is a ticket, not a discovery blog. Why incumbents leave it. Snapshot-and-resume is how Cloud Agents feel instant. A control whose success metric is "we refused to upload 87 percent of the bytes" makes time-to-first-token look worse in the same weekly adoption review. Air's Filter detonates plugins; it does not sit on tar.

Sources: TokenStead — ZCode git upload · Cursor Cloud Agent security · YC RFS — AI-Native Compliance

2. Fin remembers the refund it already issued, including when billing says it never did

Who. VP Support or Head of CX at a Series B–D SaaS company on Fin (the Intercom suite) or Zendesk Suite plus Copilot. Budget sits with the person who already defends the $0.99-per-outcome Fin line and the Advanced/Expert seats. What's broken. The mid-September r/AI_Agents "memory trust gap" thread is the scene: the agent reads its own week-old note that the ticket is resolved, tells the customer so, and never checks Stripe. The customer escalates. Fin Memory, shipped in July, is built to do exactly that carry-forward — "pick up where you left off" across channels — and Fin still bills $0.99 when the customer stops asking. Two September papers the thread cites ("The Memory Trust Gap," "Revoked but Still Authoritative") found models trusting a stale stored fact 92–100 percent of the time and five popular memory stores leaving revoked rows live at retrieval. Paying today. X is paying Fin Advanced or Expert seats ($85–$132/full seat/month annual) plus Fin AI Agent at $0.99 per resolution / procedure handoff, often with Zendesk still in the stack as the ticket system of record. Y today. Fin was bought as the resolution agent on top of the Help Center. Content sources (Salesforce Knowledge, Confluence, Notion, Guru) re-sync on a 24-hour timer; website sync is weekly; Guidance has version history; Fin Voice can show which article answered a call. Fin Memory stores prior conversation details so the next session does not re-ask. Closest threatening feature: daily source sync plus per-conversation source citations on Voice. Those refresh articles. They do not bind a remembered fact ("refund issued 12 Sep") to a live system-of-record check with a revoked-at timestamp that retrieval must honor. Fin's outcome meter rewards the conversation that ends. It does not reward the conversation that went back to billing before answering. Intercom/Salesforce will keep improving Help Center sync. They will not ship "our own memory should have been invisible after the refund row disappeared." SaaS wedge. A memory-revocation layer in front of Fin / Zendesk Copilot: every durable agent fact carries a source system, a checked-at time, and a revoke hook; retrieval fails closed on stale or revoked rows and forces a live tool call. Per-resolution plus a share of reversed bad outcomes, attached to the Fin outcome line. Conversion test. If X is already paying Fin at $0.99 per outcome, they attach this rather than rip Fin out, because Y's job is "the customer does not repeat themselves" and this job is "a remembered refund cannot close a ticket the ledger still shows open." Unlock motion: more procedures get delegated to Fin once a stale-memory miss is an automatic reopen, not a VP Slack. Why incumbents leave it. Fin Memory exists to raise resolution rate, which is the billable event. A control whose success metric is "we refused to use last week's note" makes outcome volume look worse in the same weekly CX review.

3. The code-review subagent inherits Bash because the tools field was left blank

Who. VP Engineering or Head of Developer Experience at a company that standardized on Claude Code Team (often next to GitHub Copilot Business). Budget sits with the person who already signs those seats and GitHub Advanced Security. What's broken. Claude Code's current subagent docs are the scene: omit tools and the child inherits every parent tool, MCP servers included. Skillcrossroads' public scan of 87 subagents found 57 percent declare no tools list at all, which the authors read as the opposite of a safe default — a worker meant to "just read code" now carries Bash. Another 16 granted bare Bash and 8 granted a wildcard; 85 percent of the sample was not least-privilege. Permission prompts still fire in the parent TTY. They do not fire as a separate identity for the child. Paying today. X is paying Claude Code Team or Enterprise, usually with GitHub Copilot Business ($19+/user/month) and Advanced Security on the same org. Y today. Claude Code Team was bought as the terminal coding agent. Custom subagents take a YAML frontmatter with optional tools, disallowedTools, permissionMode, and skills. Built-in Explore/Plan already drop some tools; forks inherit the parent's exact pool. Closest threatening feature: the tools / disallowedTools fields plus /agents UI. Those are authoring conveniences. The documented default remains inherit-all, the public corpus follows that default, and there is no org policy that fails CI when a new subagent ships without an allowlist. Anthropic just taught Claude Code to read AGENTS.md. It did not add a required tool grant. GitHub Copilot's plugin pin is a marketplace SHA, not a child-agent capability set. YC's current RFS list still asks for AI-native compliance infrastructure that flags and reports this class of drift. SaaS wedge. A subagent-grant linter and runtime: fail the PR when a Claude Code / Copilot / Codex worker omits tools, emit an allowlist diff, and refuse to start a child whose resolved grant includes Bash or an MCP write tool unless an org policy names it. Seat plus per-repo, attached to the Claude Code / GHAS line. Conversion test. If X is already paying Claude Code Team and GHAS, they attach this rather than rip Claude Code out, because Y's job is "engineers can spawn a reviewer" and this job is "the reviewer cannot curl | bash." Unlock motion: more MCP servers get approved once a missing tools: field is a blocked merge, not an incident. Why incumbents leave it. Inherit-all is how a first-run subagent feels magical. A control whose success metric is "we refused to launch the worker" makes subagent adoption look worse in the same weekly coding-agent review. Sequoia's Air thesis covers plugin supply chain, not the YAML default that multiplies the parent's grant.

Sources: Claude Code — subagents · HN — Skillcrossroads skill lint · YC RFS · Sequoia — Air
